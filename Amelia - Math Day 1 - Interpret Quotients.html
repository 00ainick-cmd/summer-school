Interpret quotients

When you divide, you split a total into equal groups. The answer to a division problem is called a quotient. Today you learn to read what a quotient means.

By the end you will know two ways to picture 12 / 3 = 4 and pick which story matches a problem.

What this lesson teaches Interpret quotients of whole numbers.
01

What a quotient is

A quotient is the answer when you divide. In 12 / 3 = 4, the number 4 is the quotient. The 12 is the total. The 3 tells you how to share it.

One sentence: the quotient tells you how big each equal group is, or how many groups you can make.
02

Two ways to read 12 / 3

The same division problem can be pictured in two ways. Both are correct. The story you tell decides which view you use.

Sharing
12 split into 3 equal groups

"How many in each group?" The answer is 4.

Measuring
How many 3s fit in 12?

"How many groups of 3?" The answer is 4.

Heads up: in both views, the quotient is 4. The picture changes, but the answer is the same.
03

Division and multiplication are linked

Every division fact has a matching multiplication fact. If 12 / 3 = 4, then 3 x 4 = 12. You can check your quotient by multiplying.

Try the link: 20 / 4 = ?. To check, multiply your answer by 4 and see if you get 20. If you do, the quotient is right.
04

Try it: pick the right meaning

Tap the card that matches the story. The page tells you if your pick is right.

Story 1. Jada has 15 stickers. She shares them equally with 5 friends. How many stickers per friend?

15 stickers split into 5 equal groups means 15 / 5. Each group is 3.

Story 2. A bag holds 4 apples. How many bags do you need for 16 apples?

You are asking how many 4s fit in 16. The answer is 4 bags.

Story 3. Check this quotient: is 24 / 6 = 4?

Multiplication checks division. 6 x 4 = 24 proves 24 / 6 = 4.
