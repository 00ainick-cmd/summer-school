Day 2 · Week 2

Word problems for multiplication and division

Math is not only numbers. Every problem tells a story. Today you learn how to draw and describe stories that match multiplication and division.

By the end you will match drawings, words, and equations like 4 x 3 = 12 to the right kind of situation.

What this lesson teaches Describe in words or drawings a problem that illustrates a multiplication or division situation.
01

Words tell a story. Equations save space.

A word problem describes what happens. An equation shows the same idea with numbers and signs. Your job is to spot which is which.

Story: "There are 4 vases. Each vase holds 3 flowers. How many flowers in all?" The matching equation is 4 x 3 = 12.

Big idea: if a story has equal groups, the math is multiplication or division. You multiply to find the total. You divide to share or measure.
02

Four kinds of stories

Multiplication and division show up in four common kinds of word problems. Knowing the type helps you pick the right equation.

KindWhat the story looks likeExample
Equal groupsRepeated copies of the same group size.5 boxes of 4 crayons = 5 x 4.
ArrayRows and columns of equal counts.3 rows of 6 chairs = 3 x 6.
AreaA rectangle that is L long and W wide.4 by 7 floor tiles = 4 x 7.
ScalingMany times as much as a smaller amount.A pole is 3 times taller than a 2 foot post. 3 x 2 = 6 feet.
Heads up: division stories are the same four kinds, but you start with the total and find the unknown piece. Like "20 chairs in 4 rows. How many per row?" That is 20 / 4 = 5.
03

Build the equation from the story

Read the story. Spot what you have and what you want. Then write the equation.

Story A. 6 friends each ate 2 cookies. How many cookies in all?

You have 6 equal groups of 2. You want the total. Equation: 6 x 2 = 12 cookies.

Story B. A teacher had 18 books. She put them into stacks of 6. How many stacks?

You have 18 total. Each stack is 6. You want the number of groups. Equation: 18 / 6 = 3 stacks.

04

Try it: match the story to the math

Tap the choice that matches the story.

Story 1. A box has 8 rows of 5 chocolates. How many chocolates in all?

Rows and columns make an array. 8 rows of 5 means 8 x 5 = 40 chocolates.

Story 2. Liam has 24 marbles. He shares them with 3 friends, each getting the same amount. How many marbles per friend?

Sharing the total into equal groups is division. 24 / 3 = 8 marbles each.

Story 3. Which story matches 9 x 4 = 36?

9 x 4 means 9 equal groups of 4. Nine plates of 4 grapes fits the equation.
