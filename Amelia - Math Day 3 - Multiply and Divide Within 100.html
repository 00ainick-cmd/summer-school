Multiply and divide within 100

A "hundred chart" shows the numbers 1 to 100. Most multiplication and division facts you need this year live inside this chart. Today you practice using them to solve problems.

By the end you will use facts like 8 x 7 = 56 to answer real questions in a few seconds.

What this lesson teaches Use multiplication and division within 100 to solve problems.
01

What "within 100" means

The standard says use multiplication and division within 100. That means the answer (or the total you divide) is no bigger than 100. Most facts you know like 6 x 7 = 42 fit inside.

Big idea: if you know your basic facts, you can solve real problems in seconds. The numbers stay friendly.
02

Key facts to keep ready

These are the friendly facts you will use today. Each one unlocks a matching division fact.

6 x 7 = 42
42 / 6 = 7 and 42 / 7 = 6
8 x 4 = 32
32 / 8 = 4 and 32 / 4 = 8
9 x 6 = 54
54 / 9 = 6 and 54 / 6 = 9
7 x 8 = 56
56 / 7 = 8 and 56 / 8 = 7
Tip: when you see a division problem, ask yourself "What times the divisor gives me this total?" The answer is the quotient.
03

A simple plan for word problems

When a problem appears, walk through these four steps.

1Read once. Picture what is happening.
2Find the numbers. What total do you have? What groups?
3Pick the operation. Multiply to find a total. Divide to share or measure.
4Solve and check. Use a known fact. Multiply your division answer to check.
04

Try it: pick the right move

Read the story. Tap the matching equation and the answer.

Story 1. A team has 9 players. There are 4 teams. How many players in all?

Equal groups, find total. 9 x 4 = 36 players. The numbers stay within 100.

Story 2. A book has 72 pages. Maya reads 8 pages a day. How many days to finish?

How many 8s fit in 72? Nine. 72 / 8 = 9 days. Check: 9 x 8 = 72.

Story 3. A school orders 6 boxes of folders. Each box holds 8 folders. How many folders?

Equal groups, find total. 6 x 8 = 48 folders.
