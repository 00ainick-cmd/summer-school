The missing number

Sometimes an equation has a blank box where a number should be. Your job is to figure out what fits. The good news: multiplication and division are linked, so you have two ways to find it.

By the end you will solve equations like ? x 5 = 30 and 24 / ? = 6 in seconds.

What this lesson teaches Determine the unknown number in a multiplication or division equation relating three whole numbers.
01

What an unknown is

An unknown is a number you have not figured out yet. People often use a box, a blank line, or a letter to mark its place. In the equation ? x 5 = 30, the ? stands for the missing factor.

One sentence: the unknown is whatever number makes the equation true.
02

Fact families help you find it

Multiplication and division are connected. Three numbers like 6, 5, and 30 make a fact family with four equations.

6 x 5 = 30
5 x 6 = 30
30 / 5 = 6
30 / 6 = 5
How this helps: if ? x 5 = 30, you can flip the problem. Ask "30 / 5 = ?" That gives you 6.
03

Three places the blank can hide

In any multiplication or division equation, the unknown can be in one of three spots. Same trick each time: use the other two numbers.

? x 4 = 28   →   28 / 4 = 7
6 x ? = 42   →   42 / 6 = 7
36 / ? = 9   →   36 / 9 = 4
Heads up: when the blank is the divisor, swap it with the quotient. 36 / 4 also equals 9.
04

Try it: find the missing number

Tap the choice that makes the equation true.

1. ? x 6 = 48

48 / 6 = 8. Eight groups of six make 48.

2. 9 x ? = 63

63 / 9 = 7. The missing factor is 7.

3. 24 / ? = 6

Use the fact family: 4 x 6 = 24, so 24 / 4 = 6. The unknown divisor is 4.
