Two-step word problems

Some problems hide two questions inside one story. You solve the first part, then use the answer to solve the second part. The letter that stands for the missing number is called a "variable."

By the end you will break a story into Step 1 and Step 2, name the variable, and solve.

What this lesson teaches Write and solve two-step problems involving variables using any of the four operations.
01

What a two-step problem is

A one-step problem asks you to do one thing. A two-step problem asks you to do two. You finish the first step, get a number, then use that number in the second step.

Big idea: if you skip Step 1, your Step 2 has nothing to work with. Always do them in order.
02

What a variable is

A variable is a letter that stands in for a number you do not know yet. You will see n, x, or ? in many problems. When you solve the problem, you find out what number the letter equals.

Example: "Mia has 3 packs of stickers with 8 in each. She uses some and has n left." Here n is the unknown. You will work out what n equals.
03

The four-step plan

Same plan, every time.

1
Read it once. Picture the story. Notice every number.
2
Find Step 1. What can you figure out first? Write it as an equation.
3
Find Step 2. Use the Step 1 answer. Write the equation with the variable.
4
Solve and check. Did your answer match the story?
04

A worked example

A baker fills 4 boxes with 6 cookies each. She and her son eat 5 cookies. How many cookies are left? Call the answer n.

1
Step 1. Find the total baked.
4 x 6 = 24 cookies
2
Step 2. Take away the eaten ones.
24 - 5 = n
3
Solve. n = 19.
4
Check. 19 left plus 5 eaten gives 24. Yes, that matches Step 1.
The trick: the variable just labels the missing number. Solving means finding what it equals.
05

Try it: pick the right Step 1

Read the story. Tap the smartest first move.

1. A park has 5 benches with 4 seats each. Today 3 seats are broken. How many good seats are there? Call it n.

First find total seats, then subtract the broken ones. 5 x 4 = 20, then 20 - 3 = 17.

2. Liam reads 6 pages each day for 7 days. Then he reads 4 more pages. How many pages in all? Call it n.

Equal groups means multiply. 6 x 7 = 42, then add the 4 extra. n = 46.

3. There are 36 markers. Mia shares them equally into 4 cups. Then she puts 2 more in each cup. How many in each cup? Call it n.

Share into 4 cups means divide. 36 / 4 = 9, then add 2 more. n = 11.
