Fractions on a number line

A number line is a straight road of numbers. Fractions live on this road too. To put them there, you split the space from 0 to 1 into equal jumps. Each tick mark is a fraction.

By the end you can read a number line and name the fraction at a marked spot.

What this lesson teaches Represent fractions on a number line.
01

Start with 0 to 1

A fraction lives between 0 and 1 on the number line. You draw a line, mark 0 on the left and 1 on the right. Everything between them is the whole.

Big idea: the space from 0 to 1 stands for one whole. You split that space into equal jumps to show fractions.
02

The bottom number tells you how to split

If you want to show fourths, you split the space from 0 to 1 into four equal jumps. The first tick after 0 is 1/4. The next is 2/4. Then 3/4. The end is 4/4, which is the same spot as 1.

A line split into fourths
Four equal jumps. Each jump is 1/4. The tick after one jump is 1/4. The tick after three jumps is 3/4.
03

The top number counts the jumps

To find 3/4 on the line, start at 0 and count three jumps. You land on the third tick mark. That spot is 3/4.

A line split into sixths
Six equal jumps. The dot lands after 3 jumps. That spot is 3/6.
Quick tip: the bottom number tells how many jumps fit between 0 and 1. The top number tells how many jumps you take from 0.
04

Where 0 and 1 sit

0 is the start of the whole. 1 is the end of the whole. Every fraction with the same top and bottom (like 4/4 or 6/6) sits right on top of 1, because all the jumps together make one whole.

Big idea: 0/4 is at 0. 4/4 is at 1. 2/4 is in the middle. Same idea for any other bottom number.
05

Try it: read the dot

Look at the line, count the jumps from 0, and tap the right fraction.

1. A line is split into 4 equal jumps. The dot sits on the 2nd tick after 0. What fraction is the dot at?

Four equal jumps means the bottom is 4. The dot is on the 2nd tick after 0, so the top is 2. The fraction is 2/4.

2. A line is split into 3 equal jumps. The dot sits on the 1st tick after 0.

Three equal jumps gives the bottom 3. The dot is one jump from 0. The fraction is 1/3.

3. A line is split into 8 equal jumps. The dot sits on the 5th tick after 0.

Eight equal jumps gives the bottom 8. The dot is five jumps from 0. The fraction is 5/8.
