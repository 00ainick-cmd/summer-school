Chapter one

Illustrations Tell Stories

Pictures in a book are not just there to look pretty. They are part of the story. The words say one thing. The picture often shows more. Mood. Feelings. Details the words leave out. A good reader uses both.

By the end you will explain what an illustration adds that the words alone do not give you.

What this lesson teachesExplain how specific aspects of a text's illustrations contribute to what is conveyed by the words in a story.
01

Words and pictures work together

In a story, the words tell you what happens. The picture shows you how it looks and how it feels. Sometimes the picture even shows a small thing the words skip over. A good reader checks both.

The words say
"Maya stepped onto the stage."
What you get: Maya did the action. She is on a stage.
The picture shows
Maya's eyes are wide. Her hands are tight at her sides. The stage lights are bright.
What you get: Maya is nervous. The room is loud and bright. That feeling is not in the words.
02

Three jobs a picture can do

A picture in a story can do three big jobs. Each one helps you understand more than the words alone.

Show the mood
Dark sky, gray colors, low light. The picture feels sad or scary even before you read the words.
Show feelings
A face can show worry, joy, or fear. The words might just say "she walked in." The face fills in the rest.
Add details
The words may not say what someone is wearing or what is in the room. The picture shows it.
03

Try it: what does the picture add?

Read the words. Then read what the picture shows. Pick what the picture adds. Each one has three tries to walk you through it.

04

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
If I read the words, I do not need to look at the picture.
No. The picture is part of the story. It can show the mood, a feeling, or a detail the words left out. Skipping the picture is like skipping a paragraph.
