Chapter two

Decoding Multisyllabic Words

Big words look scary until you break them into beats. Every syllable is one beat, one mouth opening. Find the beats and the word reads itself.

By the end you will name the six syllable types, find a syllable boundary, and sound out long words like fantastic and important.

What this lesson teachesDecoding multisyllabic words in context, and independent of context, by applying common spelling patterns.
01

The six syllable types

Every syllable has one vowel sound. The letters around that vowel tell you which type it is.

02

Where to cut the word

When two vowels have consonants between them, the consonants tell you where to cut. These three patterns cover most long words.

VC / CV
rab/bit
Two consonants in the middle: split between them. Most common.
V / CV
o/pen
One consonant: usually goes with the next vowel. First syllable stays open and long.
VC / V
cab/in
Sometimes the consonant stays with the first vowel. First syllable is closed and short.
03

Split the word

Tap a spot between two letters to drop a syllable cut. Get every beat. Then read each beat out loud.

Tap between letters to place a syllable cut.
04

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
A long word has to be one whole sound.
No. A word like fantastic is three small words stuck together: fan + tas + tic. Each beat has one vowel sound. Read the beats, then run them together.
