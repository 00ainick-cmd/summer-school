Chapter two

Monitor Your Comprehension

Good readers do a small check while they read. They ask, "Am I getting this?" When the answer is no, they stop. They do something to fix it. Then they keep going. That little check is called monitoring.

By the end you will notice when reading stops making sense. You will pick a fix-up move and try it.

What this lesson teachesMonitoring comprehension and making corrections and adjustments when understanding breaks down.
01

Catch the moment things stop making sense

You may not notice it at first, but your brain is telling you. Watch for these small signs while you read.

Your eyes drift away
You read three lines and could not say what they were about.
A word stops you
You hit a word you do not know and the sentence falls apart.
You feel lost
A new name or place pops up. You are not sure how it fits.
The story stops making sense
Two parts seem to say the opposite thing.
02

Three fix-up moves

When the reading breaks down, do not just keep going. Try one of these fix-up moves. Then read on.

Move 1
Reread
Go back and read the part again. Read slower. Most fixes happen here.
Move 2
Look up
If one word is the problem, look it up or use the words around it to guess.
Move 3
Ask or check
Ask yourself a question. Check the picture, the title, or earlier parts of the story.
03

Try it: pick the right fix-up move

Read each short passage. The tricky part is marked. Pick the best fix-up move for that problem.

04

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
If I get lost while reading, I should just keep going.
No. If you keep going, the rest of the story gets harder. Stop. Use a fix-up move. Then keep going. That is what good readers do every day.
