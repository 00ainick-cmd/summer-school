Chapter three

Draw Conclusions with Evidence

A story does not say everything out loud. The author drops clues and trusts you to figure out the rest. That figuring out is a conclusion, and a good conclusion always points back to a sentence that proves it.

By the end you will draw a conclusion from a short story and find the exact sentence that supports it.

What this lesson teachesDraw conclusions and support with textual evidence.
01

What a conclusion is

A conclusion is the thing you figure out that the author did not say outright. You hold it up next to a sentence from the story and say, "See? Right here." That sentence is your evidence.

02

Strong conclusion vs weak guess

A strong conclusion has a sentence behind it. A weak guess does not.

Strong conclusion
Mia was nervous about her recital.
"Her hands shook as she set her sheet music on the stand."
Strong. Shaking hands is a clear clue, the sentence proves it.
Weak guess
Mia wished she had picked a different song.
No sentence in the story says or shows this.
Weak. It might be true, but there is no evidence.
03

Try it: pick the conclusion AND the proof

Read the short passage. First pick the conclusion that fits best. Then pick the sentence from the passage that proves it.

04

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
A conclusion is just whatever I think might be true.
No. A conclusion has to be backed by a sentence from the story. If you cannot point to the line that shows it, it is a guess, not a conclusion.
