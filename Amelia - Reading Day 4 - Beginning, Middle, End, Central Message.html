Chapter four

Beginning, Middle, End, and the Central Message

Every story has bones underneath it. The setup, the trouble, the way out. If you can find those three beats, you have the summary. And once you have the summary, you can ask the biggest question of all: what is the lesson here?

By the end you will summarize a short story in three sentences and pick the central message.

What this lesson teachesSummarizing a story's beginning, middle, and determining their central message, lesson or moral.
01

The bones of a story

A summary is the story shrunk to its bones. One sentence for each part.

Beginning
Setup
Who is in it, where they are, what is normal.
Middle
Problem
Something goes wrong. The character tries to fix it.
End
Solution
How it works out and what changes.
02

Plot vs central message

The plot is what happened. The central message is the lesson the story teaches. The plot is different in every story, the message often shows up again and again.

Classic message
Overcoming fear
A scared character tries anyway and finds out it was not as bad as they thought.
Classic message
Kindness pays off
A small kind act comes back around to help the character later.
Classic message
Look before you leap
A character rushes in and learns to slow down and check first.
Classic message
Practice makes you better
A character struggles, keeps at it, and improves.

04

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
The central message is the plot.
No. The plot is what happened: a girl built a stand, sales were slow, she changed her sign. The message is the lesson: trying a small change can fix a problem. The message is what the story teaches, not what it describes.
