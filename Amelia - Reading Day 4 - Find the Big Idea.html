Chapter four

Find the Big Idea

A story or paragraph is built around one big idea. That big idea is what the whole thing is mostly about. The other sentences are details that hold the big idea up. A good reader spots the big idea and says it in their own words.

By the end you will name the big idea of a short text. You will say it in your own words. You will point to two details that back it up.

What this lesson teachesParaphrase the big idea/themes and supporting details of texts.
01

Big idea vs detail

The big idea is what the whole thing is mostly about. A detail is one piece that holds it up. A big idea works for the whole text. A detail works for just one part.

Big idea
Bees do important work for the food we eat.
Detail
Bees move pollen from one flower to another.
Detail
Apples and pumpkins need bees to grow well.
Detail
About one in three foods you eat needs a bee.
02

Paraphrase: say it in your own words

When you paraphrase, you say the same idea in your own words. Not word for word from the book. Not longer than it needs to be. Just the heart of it.

The text says

"The library hosts a free reading hour every Saturday so kids in the neighborhood can come share their favorite books."

Your paraphrase

The library has a free hour on Saturdays for kids to come share books they love.

Same big idea. Your words. A bit shorter. That is the move.

03

Try it: pick the big idea, then the details

Read each short passage. First pick the big idea. Then pick a detail that supports it.

04

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
A small fact in the text is the big idea.
No. A small fact is a detail. The big idea is what the whole text is about. If your "big idea" only fits one sentence, it is really a detail. Zoom out.
