Chapter five

Author's Purpose

Every author writes for a reason. Some want to teach you a fact. Some want to talk you into doing something. Some want to make you laugh or feel something. Spotting that reason helps you read better. Use the word PIE: Persuade, Inform, Entertain.

By the end you will name the author's purpose for a short text and pick the clue that tells you.

What this lesson teachesExplain the author's purpose.
01

PIE: three reasons authors write

Authors usually have one big reason for writing. Three letters help you remember them. P for persuade. I for inform. E for entertain.

P
Persuade
Try to talk you into something. Get you to agree or do something.
I
Inform
Teach you facts. Tell you how something works.
E
Entertain
Tell a fun story. Make you laugh, feel, or wonder.
02

How to tell which is which

Every purpose leaves clues on the page. Learn the clues and you can name the purpose fast.

Inform clues
  • Real facts and dates
  • Headings and labels
  • Sounds like a textbook
  • Lots of "is" and "are"
Persuade clues
  • "You should ..."
  • "The best way ..."
  • Strong opinion words
  • Reasons to agree
Entertain clues
  • Made-up characters
  • "Once there was ..."
  • Feelings and surprise
  • Funny or scary parts
03

Try it: name the purpose

Read each short passage. Pick the author's purpose. Then pick the line that tells you.

04

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
If a text has facts in it, it must be there to inform.
Not always. A persuade text may use a fact or two to back up its opinion. The key is the whole purpose. Ask: is the author trying to tell me what is true, talk me into something, or tell a story? The strongest clue wins.
