Chapter five

Details That Support the Main Idea

In a nonfiction paragraph, one sentence carries the whole point. The others are there to prove it. Once you can spot the main idea, the details either back it up or they wander off.

By the end you will find the main idea sentence in a short paragraph, tag the details that support it, and notice the off topic sentence that sneaks in.

What this lesson teachesIdentify the details or facts that support the main idea.
01

The umbrella idea

Picture the main idea as an umbrella. The supporting details are the things standing under it. Each detail should fit under the umbrella, not point somewhere else.

Honeybees help plants grow.
DetailThey carry pollen from flower to flower.
DetailMany crops need bee pollen to make fruit.
DetailOne bee can visit hundreds of flowers a day.
02

Detail vs off topic

A detail proves the main idea. An off topic sentence is true, but it does not fit under the umbrella. Watch for these. They are the sneaky ones.

Spot it
Main idea: Honeybees help plants grow.
Detail: "They carry pollen from flower to flower." Fits under the umbrella.
Off topic: "Honey tastes good on toast." True, but it is about toast, not plants growing.
03

Try it: tag the sentences

For each paragraph, tag the main idea sentence, the supporting details, and any off topic sentence that sneaks in.

MI = main ideaD = detailO = off topic
04

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
The first sentence is always the main idea.
Usually, but not always. The main idea is the one big thing the whole paragraph is about. Sometimes it shows up in the middle or even the end. Always ask: what is the umbrella all the other sentences fit under?
