Week three, Day one

Context Clues for New Words

When you hit a word you do not know, do not stop. The sentence around it is doing work for you. Look at the words next to it. They give hints. A good reader picks up those hints and guesses what the new word means.

By the end you will spot a new word, find the clue words around it, and pick the meaning that fits.

What this lesson teachesUsing sentence level context to determine the relevant meaning of unfamiliar words or distinguish among multiple meaning words.
01

What a context clue is

A context clue is a word or a piece of a sentence that helps you figure out what a new word means. You do not need a dictionary every time. Most of the time the sentence already tells you. You just have to look for it.

Example
The hike was arduous, very hard and tiring, and we had to stop to rest.
Clue: "very hard and tiring." So arduous means hard or tough.
02

Three kinds of clues to look for

Sentences hide clues in three main ways. Once you know the shapes, you spot them fast.

Meaning clue. The sentence almost gives you the meaning. Look for words like "or," "which means," or commas. Example: The wolf was famished, or very hungry, after the long hunt.
Example clue. The sentence lists examples. Example: She loves citrus fruits like oranges, lemons, and limes. So citrus means a kind of fruit like those.
Opposite clue. The sentence shows the opposite. Example: Sam is usually loud, but today he was timid, not loud at all. So timid means shy or quiet.
03

Words with more than one meaning

Some words have more than one meaning. The word bark can be a dog sound, or the rough skin of a tree. The sentence around it tells you which one fits. You pick the meaning that makes the sentence make sense.

Sentence A
The puppy gave a sharp bark when the door opened.
Here bark means a dog sound. (A puppy at a door fits that meaning.)
Sentence B
The bark of the old oak tree was rough and gray.
Here bark means the rough skin of a tree. (Same word, different meaning.)
04

Try it: read, find clues, pick the meaning

Read each short passage. The new word is in red. The clues are in green. Pick the meaning that fits.

05

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
You just guess based on how the word sounds.
No. A word can sound like one thing and mean another. Famished sounds a little like famous, but they are not close in meaning. Use the sentence, not the sound. Read the words right before and right after the new word, and pick the meaning that makes the sentence make sense.
