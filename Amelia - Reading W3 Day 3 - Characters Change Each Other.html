Characters Change Each Other

Characters in a story do not just stand near each other. They push and pull on each other. A friend can make a shy kid braver. A rival can make a calm kid grumpy. A mentor can teach a beginner how to try. The way two people act around each other shapes the story.

By the end you will name how two characters relate, and you will show how one of them changed because of the other.

What this lesson teachesDescribe the interaction of characters, including relationships and how they change.
01

What a relationship is

A relationship is how two characters stand to each other. Are they friends? Family? Rivals? Teacher and student? The kind of relationship shapes how they act in every scene.

Common kinds of story relationships:

friendfamilyrivalmentorteammatestrangerneighborhelper

A mentor is someone older or more skilled who teaches a younger person. A rival is someone you are trying to beat or who is trying to beat you.

02

How characters change each other

When two characters spend time together, one often changes the other. The change is in how they act, feel, or think. Look for these clues: a character does something new, drops an old habit, talks in a new way, or feels something they did not feel before.

Tara never raised her hand in class. Then a new kid, Ana, sat next to her. Ana asked questions out loud all the time. After a few weeks, Tara started raising her hand too. By the end of the month, Tara even asked the first question.

Before

Tara never raised her hand.

After

Tara asked the first question.

Ana is a friend who acts like a model. Tara changed because of her. That is the interaction.

03

Three signs of change

Sign 1, new action
The character does something they did not do before.
"Tara raised her hand for the first time."
Sign 2, new feeling
The character has a feeling they did not have before.
"Sam felt proud after winning, but only because his coach trusted him."
Sign 3, new way of talking
The character speaks in a new way to people.
"Mia used to grumble. After working with Mr. Lee, she said please and thank you."
04

Try it: name the relationship, then the change

Read each short story. First pick the relationship. Then pick how one character changed because of the other.

05

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
A change in mood is the same as a real change in the character.
No. A bad day can make anyone grumpy for an hour. A real change means the character keeps acting in a new way after the moment. Look for the new habit or the new choice, not just the feeling of one scene.
