Text Features Help You Find

Nonfiction books have more than words. They have titles, headings, bold words, photos, captions, and lists. Those are text features. They are not just for show. They help you find what you need fast and check if your guess about the topic is right.

By the end you will name common text features, use them to find info, and use them to check a prediction.

What this lesson teachesUse text and graphic features to locate information and to make and verify predictions.
01

Common text features

A text feature is a part of a nonfiction page that is not just words in a line. Each one does a job for the reader.

Title
Big words at the top. Tells you the topic of the whole book or page.
Use it for: What is the page about?
Heading
Words above a chunk of text. Tells you what that part is about.
Use it for: Skipping to the part you need.
Bold word
A word in dark print. Tells you the word is important and often has a meaning to learn.
Use it for: Spotting key words to learn.
Photo with caption
A picture with a short line under it. The caption tells you what the picture shows.
Use it for: Quick info without reading a whole paragraph.
Table of contents
A list at the front. Tells you which chapter is on which page.
Use it for: Going straight to the chapter you want.
02

Use features to find info fast

Say you need to know what otters eat. You do not have to read the whole book. Try this:

  1. Use the table of contents to find a chapter about food.
  2. Look at headings on each page to skip to the right part.
  3. Scan for bold words like "diet" or "prey."
  4. Read the caption under a photo of an otter eating.

In one minute you can find the answer. That is text features doing the work.

03

Use features to make and check a prediction

A prediction is a smart guess about what a book is going to say. You make one before you read. Then you check it.

All About Bats
Chapter 2: Where Bats Sleep

Bats sleep upside down. They hold on tight with their claws. Most bats live in caves or in old trees.

photo of a bat hanging upside down in a cave
Photo. A small bat sleeping in a cave.

Before you read, the title and heading tell you the topic. You can predict: "I bet this part is going to tell me where bats sleep and how they hold on." After you read, check the photo, caption, and bold word. If they match your guess, your prediction was right. If they show something new, change your prediction.

04

Try it: use the features

Look at the sample page in each round. Pick which feature you would use to answer the question. Then check a prediction with another feature.

05

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
If you have not read the whole page, you cannot answer questions about it.
No. Text features are made for fast finding. The heading, photo, and caption can give you the answer in seconds. Use them on purpose. Reading the whole page is great too, but you do not always need to.
