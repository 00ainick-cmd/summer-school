Week three, Day five

Fact and Opinion

Nonfiction is full of two kinds of lines. Facts and opinions. A fact can be checked and proved. An opinion tells how a person feels or thinks. Both can be useful. A good reader tells them apart so they know what to trust and what to weigh.

By the end you will pick fact from opinion in a sentence, and you will say why.

What this lesson teachesDistinguish fact from opinion.
01

Facts and opinions, side by side

Fact

"Mars is a planet."

Why: you can check this with a science book or a teacher. It is true for everyone.

Opinion

"Mars is the best planet."

Why: "Best" is a feeling. Another person could say Jupiter is the best. Cannot be proved.

Both sentences talk about Mars. One can be checked. The other tells you what someone feels.

02

Signal words for opinions

Some words tip you off that a line is an opinion. Watch for these:

bestworstI thinkI feelshouldalwaysmost funcutestamazingboring

These words tell you a feeling, not a fact. "Dogs are the best pet" is an opinion. "Dogs have four legs" is a fact.

03

The test: can it be proved?

When you are not sure, ask this one question:

"Could two people check this and agree?"
If yes, it is a fact. If two people might never agree, it is an opinion.

Try it: fact

"The library opens at 9 a.m."

Check the sign or the website. Anyone would get the same answer.

Try it: opinion

"The library is the most fun place in town."

Some kids love the pool. Some love the library. Cannot be proved.
04

Try it: sort fact from opinion

Read each short passage. Pick the sentence that is a fact. Then pick the sentence that is an opinion.

05

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
If a lot of people agree with a line, it is a fact.
No. Lots of kids might say chocolate is the best ice cream, but that is still an opinion. A fact has to be checked and proved with the same answer every time. Popular is not the same as true.
