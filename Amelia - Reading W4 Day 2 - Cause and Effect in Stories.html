Cause and Effect in Stories

One thing in a story makes the next thing happen. The first thing is the cause. The thing that follows is the effect. Rain falls, so you grab a coat. The bell rings, so you stand up. When you spot the cause, you understand why the story moves the way it does.

By the end you will name the cause and the effect in any short story you read.

What this lesson teachesExplain cause and effect relationships
01

The two parts

A cause is what makes something else happen. The effect is what happens because of it. The cause comes first. The effect comes after.

Cause

The lights went out.

Effect

The class got quiet.

Ask yourself: "What happened?" That is the effect. Then ask: "Why did it happen?" That is the cause.

02

Words that point to cause and effect

Writers use signal words to link a cause to an effect. Watch for these:

Cause
because, so, since, then
Effect

Example: "Lia spilled her juice, so her sleeve was sticky." The word "so" tells you that spilling caused the sticky sleeve.

Example: "Mia could not play because her foot hurt." The word "because" points to the cause. The hurt foot caused her to stay out of the game.

03

Sometimes there are no signal words

Not every story uses "because" or "so." You still have to find the link.

Ben heard a loud bark. He jumped and dropped his book. The pages got bent.

No "so." No "because." But the chain is clear. The bark made Ben jump. Jumping made him drop the book. Dropping made the pages bend. Each step is a cause and an effect.

04

Try it: find the cause

Read each short story. The effect is in the question. Tap the cause that made it happen.

05

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
If two things happen close together, one must cause the other.
No. Two things can happen at the same time and not be linked at all. Ask yourself: "Did the first one really make the second one happen?" If you cannot say yes, it is not cause and effect.
