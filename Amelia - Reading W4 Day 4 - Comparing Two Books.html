Comparing Two Books

Two books can be best friends or total strangers. They can tell different stories but share the same big idea. They can use the same word in two ways. Smart readers do not stop at one book. They link a new book to ones they read before. That makes both books bigger.

By the end you will name what is the same and what is different between two books.

What this lesson teachesText to text (ideas and information in various fiction and nonfiction works, using compare and contrast)
01

What text to text means

A text-to-text connection is when one book reminds you of another book. The two books can be both fiction. They can both be nonfiction. One can be a story and the other a fact book. They share an idea, or they tell about the same kind of thing in a different way.

Same big idea

One book is about a kid who feels left out at school. Another book is about a frog who feels left out in a pond. Different stories, same big idea.

Same topic, different facts

One book lists facts about sharks. Another book lists facts about whales. Both are about big sea animals. The facts are not the same.

02

Compare: what is the same

When you compare, you look for what the two books share. Look for the same:

  • Topic. What both books are about.
  • Theme. The big idea both books leave you with.
  • Setting. Where or when the action happens.
  • Fact. A fact one book gives that the other book also gives.

Book 1: The Lost Puppy

A kid finds a lost puppy and helps it get home.

Book 2: All About Dogs

A fact book that tells how dogs find their owners.

What is the same: Both books are about dogs. Both share the idea that dogs and people help each other.

03

Contrast: what is different

When you contrast, you look for what is not the same. Look for:

  • Type. One could be a story, the other a fact book.
  • Characters. The people or animals in the books.
  • Plot. What events happen in each.
  • Detail. A fact one has but the other does not.

Using the same books from above, what is different: The Lost Puppy has a kid, a plot, and a problem to solve. All About Dogs has no characters and no plot. It is a list of true facts.

04

Try it: same or different

Read each pair of book clues. Tap whether the question points to Same in both, Different, or Not in either.

05

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
Two books that look alike on the cover must be the same inside.
No. Covers can match by color or by topic, but the inside ideas can be very different. Read the inside, not the outside, to compare.
