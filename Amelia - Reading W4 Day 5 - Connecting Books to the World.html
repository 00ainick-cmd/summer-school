Connecting Books to the World

A book is not just words on a page. A book talks about the real world. The world a kid lives in. The world on the news. The world your family lives in every day. A good reader links the book to real things, real people, real places. Then the book sticks.

By the end you will link an idea in a book to a real-world thing you know about.

What this lesson teachesText to world (text ideas regarding experiences in the world)
01

What text to world means

A text-to-world connection links an idea in a book to something that is happening in the real world. Not your own life. Not another book. The whole world. News. People in other places. Things you see on the way to school.

Book idea
A kid in a story plants a tree to help his town breathe cleaner air.
World link
In real life, towns plant trees in parks to clean the air people breathe.
02

Three other kinds of connections

There are three main kinds of connections readers make. Text to world is one of them.

Text to self

The book reminds you of YOU. "My grandma also bakes pies."

Text to text

The book reminds you of ANOTHER book. "This story has a brave kid like Charlotte\'s Web."

Text to world
The book reminds you of something REAL going on in the WORLD. News, places, people who really live.
03

How to make a strong text-to-world link

A strong link names a real thing in the world. Not "people are nice." That is too big to count. A real thing is something you can point to.

Ben\'s class collects cans for kids who do not have enough food at home. By June, they pack 200 lunch bags.
Weak link
"Helping people is good." (too big)
Strong link
"My school had a food drive too. Real food banks all over the country help families."

A strong link names a real thing. A food drive. A food bank. Something you can point to in the real world.

04

Try it: best text-to-world link

Read each short story. Tap the strongest text-to-world connection.

05

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
Text to world means linking the book to your own life.
No. That is text to self. Text to world links the book to something happening in the wider world: real places, real news, real kinds of people who live their lives every day.
