Investigation 02

Water Changes State

The same water can be a hard solid, a flowing liquid, or an invisible gas. Temperature is the switch. Heat it up, the water climbs through the states. Cool it down, it climbs back.

What this lesson teachesPredict and investigate that water can change from a liquid to a solid (freeze), and back again (melt), or from a liquid to a gas (evaporation), and back again (condensation) as the result of temperature changes.
01

Three states, one substance

Water is water. It just wears three different outfits depending on the temperature.

02

Four transitions, four names

A transition is a state change. Each one has a name, and each one happens at a specific temperature with water.

solid → liquid
Melt
Happens at 0°C. Ice cube on the counter turns into water.
liquid → solid
Freeze
Happens at 0°C. Water in the freezer turns into ice cubes.
liquid → gas
Evaporate
Happens fast at 100°C. A puddle in the sun slowly dries up.
gas → liquid
Condense
Your warm breath fogs a cold window. Water gas turns back into drops.
03

Temperature is the cause

Temperature tells you how much heat energy the water has. Add heat, the water speeds up and changes to a less stuck state (solid into liquid, liquid into gas). Take heat away, the water slows down and changes back (gas into liquid, liquid into solid). At 0°C water freezes or melts. At 100°C water boils away as gas. Those two numbers are the doorways.

06

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
Water gas means steam you can see.
The cloud you see above a boiling pot is tiny liquid drops that already condensed in the cool air. True water gas (water vapor) is invisible. It is in the air around you right now.
07

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
Melting and evaporating are the same thing.
Both add heat, but they are different transitions. Melting goes solid to liquid at 0°C. Evaporating goes liquid to gas, fastest at 100°C.
