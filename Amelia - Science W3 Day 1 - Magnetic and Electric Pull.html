Day 1 . Week 3

Magnetic and electric pull

Some things pull on each other without touching. Magnets do this. So does static from a balloon. The force jumps across the gap.

By the end you can name the cause and the effect when magnets or static act across a space.

What this lesson teaches Plan and conduct investigations to determine the cause and effect relationship of electric or magnetic interactions between two objects not in contact with each other.
01

Forces that act at a distance

Most pushes and pulls need touching. You push a door. Your hand has to touch it. But some forces can act across a gap. A magnet can pull on a paper clip from an inch away. A balloon you just rubbed on your hair can stick to a wall without glue.

Big idea. Two objects do not have to touch for a force to act between them.
02

Magnets have two ends

A bar magnet has a north pole (N) and a south pole (S). Put two magnets near each other. They will pull together or push apart, based on the poles you face.

Attract: pull together

  • N to S pulls in
  • S to N pulls in
  • The magnets snap together when close

Repel: push apart

  • N to N pushes out
  • S to S pushes out
  • The magnets shove away when close
Rule. Opposite poles attract. Same poles repel. The closer the magnets, the stronger the force.
03

Static can pull too

Rub a balloon on your hair for ten seconds. Hold the balloon near your hair. Your hair will lift up and reach for the balloon. The two never touched, but a force pulled them. That force is called static electricity. It is an electric pull across a gap.

How. Rubbing moves tiny charges from your hair to the balloon. Once the charges are split, the balloon and your hair pull on each other.
04

Plan a test: cause and effect

A good test changes one thing and watches what happens. The thing you change is the cause. The thing that happens is the effect.

Change: bring magnet closer. Watch: paper clip jumps to magnet.
ATTRACT
Change: flip second magnet so N faces N. Watch: magnets push apart.
REPEL
Change: rub balloon on hair, then hold near small paper bits. Watch: bits jump up to the balloon.
ATTRACT
Change: line up S to N on two magnets. Watch: they snap together.
ATTRACT
Change: line up S to S on two magnets. Watch: they slide away.
REPEL
Why one thing at a time. If you change two things, you cannot tell which one caused the effect.
05

Try one

You hold two bar magnets with their N poles facing each other. You push them closer. What happens?

Same poles repel. N facing N pushes away. The closer you push them, the harder they fight back.
