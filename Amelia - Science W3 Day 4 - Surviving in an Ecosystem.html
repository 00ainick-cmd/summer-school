Day 4 . Week 3

Surviving in an ecosystem

An ecosystem is a place with living things and the air, water, and soil they share. The same forest can be easy for one animal, hard for another, and impossible for a third.

By the end you can argue from evidence why some organisms thrive, some struggle, and some cannot survive in a given place.

What this lesson teaches Construct an argument with evidence that in a particular ecosystem some organisms - based on structural adaptations or behaviors - can survive well, some survive less well, and some cannot survive at all.
01

What an ecosystem is

An ecosystem is a place plus all the living and nonliving things in it. A forest has trees, animals, soil, water, and air. A desert has cactus, lizards, sand, and almost no water. Each ecosystem has its own rules.

Key idea. An organism's traits decide how well it fits the rules of its ecosystem. Traits are body parts (like fur, claws, or thick skin) and behaviors (like sleeping by day or running fast).
02

Three outcomes in a forest

Picture one forest. Three organisms show up. They do not all do equally well. Watch what each one's traits let it do.

Squirrel: SURVIVES WELL
It has sharp claws for climbing trees, sharp teeth for cracking nuts, and a bushy tail for balance. The forest has many trees and many nuts. Its traits match this ecosystem.
Rabbit: SURVIVES LESS WELL
It is fast on the ground and eats grass and bark, so it can stay alive. But it cannot climb trees to escape, and most of the food is up high. It survives, but not as well as the squirrel.
Cactus: CANNOT SURVIVE
A cactus is built to save water and live in hot sun. The forest is wet, cool, and shaded. The cactus would rot in wet soil and die without enough sun. Its traits do not fit this ecosystem.
03

Argue from evidence

A claim is what you think. Evidence is the proof. To argue, you say the claim and then say the body parts or behaviors that prove it.

Pattern. "This organism (claim: survives / less / cannot) because (evidence: trait that fits or does not fit the ecosystem)."
A fish in a pond. It has gills to breathe water and fins to swim.
SURVIVES WELL
A fish placed on dry land. Gills cannot get oxygen from air.
CANNOT SURVIVE
A polar bear in the Arctic. Thick fur and fat keep it warm.
SURVIVES WELL
A polar bear in a hot jungle. Thick fur and fat make it overheat.
CANNOT SURVIVE
A turtle in a pond. It can swim and rest in the sun on a log.
SURVIVES WELL
A turtle in a desert. It can dig in the sand, but the heat and dry air are hard on it.
SURVIVES LESS
04

Why outcomes split three ways

In any ecosystem, three things can happen to an organism. Some traits fit very well, so the organism thrives. Some traits fit a little, so it gets by. Some traits do not fit at all, so the organism cannot survive. The reason is always the match between traits and the ecosystem.

05

Try one

A fish lives in a clean pond. It has gills, fins, and a streamlined body. A flood washes it onto dry land. What is the most likely outcome?

Gills work in water, not in air. The fish's body parts do not match dry land, so it cannot survive there. This is evidence that traits decide survival in a given ecosystem.
