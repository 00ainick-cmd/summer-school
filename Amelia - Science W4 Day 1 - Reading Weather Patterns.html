Reading weather patterns

Weather is what happens outside on one day. A pattern is what happens many days in a row. When you put weather days into a table or a bar chart, you can spot the pattern for a whole season.

By the end you can read a weather table and a bar chart, and use them to tell what kind of weather a season usually brings.

What this lesson teaches Represent data in tables and graphical displays to describe typical weather conditions expected during a particular season.
01

Weather, season, and pattern

Weather is what the sky and air do on one day. It can be sunny, rainy, hot, cold, or windy. A season is a part of the year. There are four. Winter, spring, summer, and fall. A pattern is a thing that happens over and over.

Key idea. One day is just weather. Many days together show you a pattern. Each season has its own pattern of weather.
02

Put weather in a table

A table is a chart with rows and columns. You can use a table to log many days of weather. Look at the table below. It shows the average high temperature and the average rain for each season in a Missouri town.

SeasonAvg high (F)Avg rain (inches per month)Most common sky
Winter352Cloudy, some snow
Spring624Rainy with sun breaks
Summer853Sunny and hot
Fall653Cool and clear
How to read a table. Pick a row (the season). Slide your eye across to read each column. Winter shows 35F and snow. Summer shows 85F and sun. The table makes the pattern easy to see.
03

Put weather in a bar chart

A bar chart turns the numbers from a table into bars. A taller bar means a bigger number. Look at the four temperature bars in the hero image above. The tallest bar is summer (85F). The shortest is winter (35F). At a glance, you can see how hot or cold each season usually is.

Why bars help. Numbers are easy to mix up. Bars are not. Your eye spots the biggest and smallest right away. That is why scientists use charts to show patterns.

Try one

A scientist logs five days of weather. All five days are sunny. The high is 87F, 84F, 86F, 88F, and 85F. Which season is most likely?
