Weather hazards and safety

A hazard is a thing that can cause harm. Some kinds of weather are hazards. A flood, a tornado, and a lightning storm can all hurt people and break buildings. People design solutions to lower the risk. Your job is to look at a design and judge how well it works.

By the end you can name common weather hazards and tell which design solutions help the most and why.

What this lesson teaches Evaluate a design solution that reduces the impacts of a weather-related hazard.
01

What a weather hazard is

Weather is the air, sky, and water around you. Most days, weather is safe. But sometimes weather can hurt people or break buildings. That kind of weather is called a hazard. Three big ones in Missouri are tornadoes, floods, and lightning storms.

Key idea. A hazard is harm waiting to happen. The hazard itself does not always cause harm. The harm depends on what people do to get ready.
02

What a design solution is

A design solution is a thing people build or plan to lower the risk of harm. A basement safe room is a design solution for tornadoes. A house on tall stilts is a design solution for floods. A lightning rod is a design solution for lightning.

The pattern. Find the hazard. Find what it can do (toss things, soak things, set things on fire). Build something that blocks or guides that effect.
03

Three pairs to study

Each pair shows a hazard, what it does, and the design that lowers the harm.

Tornado . Basement safe room
A tornado's strong wind can throw cars and pull off roofs. A safe room sits below ground. The dirt and concrete around it block the wind. People inside stay safe even if the house above is hit.
Flood . House on tall stilts
A flood lifts dirty river water into homes. A house built on tall posts sits above the flood line. Water flows under instead of through. The home and the people inside stay dry.
Lightning . Lightning rod on the roof
Lightning is a huge spark of electricity. A metal rod on top of the roof catches the bolt and sends it down a wire into the ground. The rod gives the lightning an easy path that misses the people inside.
04

How to judge a design

To judge a design, ask two questions. Does it work for THIS hazard? Does it lower the harm a lot or only a little? A strong solution matches the hazard and lowers the harm a lot. A weak solution either does not match the hazard or only helps a little.

For a tornado: a basement safe room with thick walls.
STRONG
For a tornado: opening a window to let the wind out.
WEAK
For a flood: building the house on tall stilts above the flood line.
STRONG
For a flood: putting a small fence around the yard.
WEAK
For lightning: a metal rod on the roof wired to the ground.
STRONG
For lightning: standing under a tall tree to stay dry.
WEAK
05

Try one

A town gets bad tornadoes. The mayor wants one solution. Which design is the STRONGEST?

A basement safe room blocks the wind and flying things that a tornado throws. It matches the hazard and lowers the harm a lot.
