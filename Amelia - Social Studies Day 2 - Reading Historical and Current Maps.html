Reading Historical and Current Maps

A map is a snapshot of a place at one moment. A historical map shows the place long ago. A current map shows it today. Set them side by side and you can see what changed.

What this lesson teachesRead and construct historical and current maps.
01

First, find the date and source

Every good map tells you when it was made and who made it. Those clues live in the corner. You see a date, a title, and a source. Without them, you cannot tell a historical map from a current one.

Title
What the map shows.
Date
When the map was made.
Source
Who made it.
Legend
What the colors and marks mean.

A snapshot map shows a place at one date. An event map shows what happened. It might have arrows for a battle or a trip. Both kinds can be historical or current. The date in the corner tells you which.

02

Compare the two United States

Both maps show the United States. One was drawn around 1800. The country had only 16 states. They all sat on the east coast. The other map shows the country today. It reaches all the way to the Pacific. Tap a region to see its label. Then answer the questions.

03

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
An old map is not a wrong map.
The 1800 map is right for 1800. It does not show California. That is because the United States did not have California yet. Always read the date before you judge a map.
