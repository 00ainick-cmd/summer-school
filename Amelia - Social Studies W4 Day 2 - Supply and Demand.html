Week 4 . Day 2

Supply and Demand

A store sets a price. The price is not just a guess. Two things push it up or down. The first is supply, which is how much there is. The second is demand, which is how many people want it. Together they decide the price tag.

By the end you can tell what supply means, what demand means, and how each one moves the price up or down.

What this lesson teaches Explain how supply and demand affect the price of goods and services.
01

Two basic words

Before you can talk about price, you need two words. They show up in every store, every market, and every farmer stand. The whole lesson is built on them.

Word 1

Supply

Supply is how much of a thing a store or maker has ready to sell. A bakery with 100 loaves of bread has a big supply. A bakery with 5 loaves has a small supply.

Word 2

Demand

Demand is how many people want to buy a thing. If 200 people line up for cupcakes, demand is high. If only 3 people walk by, demand is low.

02

How supply moves the price

Picture a farm stand with many baskets of strawberries. The farmer has more than the buyers can eat. To get rid of all of them before they go bad, the farmer cuts the price. Now picture a stand with just one basket left. The farmer knows it is the only one. The price goes up.

SUPPLY UP

Lots to sell

When supply is big, sellers drop the price so more people will buy. Price goes DOWN.

SUPPLY DOWN

Only a few left

When supply is small, sellers can charge more for each one. Price goes UP.

03

How demand moves the price

Now think about a new toy that every kid wants. Stores see the line out the door. They know people will pay more. The price goes up. The opposite is also true. If a toy comes out and almost nobody asks for it, stores drop the price to clear it off the shelf.

DEMAND UP

Everyone wants it

When demand is high, sellers can charge more. Price goes UP.

DEMAND DOWN

Few people want it

When demand is low, sellers drop the price to attract buyers. Price goes DOWN.

04

Real life scenes

Watch the rule in action.

Scene 1. A bookstore orders 500 copies of a new book, but only 20 kids in town want it. → Big supply, small demand. Price goes DOWN. The store puts the book on sale.
Scene 2. A bakery makes 12 cupcakes for a big school event with 200 kids. → Small supply, big demand. Price goes UP. The bakery charges more for each cupcake.
Scene 3. A farmer grows so many tomatoes that crates of them sit at the stand. → Supply is high. Price goes DOWN to clear the crates.
Scene 4. A video game system comes out. Stores get 100 each. Thousands of people line up. → Demand is huge. Price goes UP. Some sellers raise the tag.
05

Tug of war

Think of price as a rope in a tug of war. Supply pulls on one side. Demand pulls on the other. When supply pulls hard, price drops. When demand pulls hard, price rises. The price ends up where both sides settle.

Big idea

Price reflects both sides

Sellers and buyers meet at a price. Lots of one thing and not many buyers means a low price. Few things and many buyers means a high price.

06

Try one

A toy store sells fidget spinners. Last summer, every kid wanted one. Stores had hardly any to sell. Many buyers wanted them. What happened to the price?

Small supply meets big demand. Both pulls move price UP. Stores can charge more because so many want one.
