Trade helps both sides

Trade is when two people swap. One gives a thing or a service. The other gives money or another thing in return. Trade only happens if both sides want it. That is the key. The buyer gets what they need. The seller gets what they need. Both walk away better than before.

By the end you can tell what trade is, who the buyer and seller are, and why each one gets a need met from one swap.

What this lesson teaches Explain how trade helps meet the needs of buyers and sellers.
01

What trade is

Trade is a swap. Two people meet. Each one has something the other wants. They swap. When the swap is done, each one has the thing they wanted. Trade is the heart of every shop, every market, and every farm stand.

Key word

Trade

Trade is the act of swapping a thing or a service for another thing or for money.

02

Two sides of every trade

Every trade has two people. They have different needs. They each bring what the other side needs.

SIDE 1

The buyer

The buyer is the person who wants a thing or a service. They bring money to swap. They take home the thing.

SIDE 2

The seller

The seller is the person who has a thing or a service ready to give. They want money. They take the money in trade.

03

Both sides win, or no trade

Trade only happens when both sides agree. If the buyer thinks the price is too high, no trade. If the seller thinks the offer is too low, no trade. When both sides say yes, both sides win. The buyer gets a need met. The seller gets a need met. That is the rule.

Big idea

One swap, two needs met

The buyer needed the thing. The seller needed money. After the swap, both have what they were missing. Trade is how needs find each other.

04

Real life scenes

Watch how each trade meets needs on both sides.

Scene 1. A kid trades $4 for a slice of pizza at the school fair. →
Buyer: gets lunch.
Seller: gets $4 to pay for flour and cheese.
Scene 2. A family pays a plumber $80 to fix a leak. →
Buyer: gets a working sink.
Seller: gets paid for their skill.
Scene 3. A farmer sells a bushel of apples to a grocery store for $30. →
Buyer (store): gets apples to sell.
Seller (farmer): gets money for the harvest.
Scene 4. Two kids trade lunch items. One gives an apple. One gives a cookie. →
Side 1: wanted a cookie, got one.
Side 2: wanted an apple, got one.
05

What if it does not feel fair

Sometimes one side does not want to swap. Maybe the buyer thinks the thing costs too much. Maybe the seller thinks the offer is too low. When that happens, no trade. Each side can walk away. That is why trade is called a free choice. Both sides have to agree.

Rule

Trade needs a YES from both sides

No yes from the buyer, no swap. No yes from the seller, no swap. Both sides have to choose.

06

Try one

Lucy pays $5 for a book at a yard sale. The book seller hands her the book. Lucy hands over $5. Which sentence is TRUE?

Lucy needed a book and got one. The seller needed money and got $5. Both sides walked away with a need met. That is how trade works.
