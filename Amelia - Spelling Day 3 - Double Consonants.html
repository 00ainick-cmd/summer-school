Chapter three

Double Consonants

Some short words double their last letter when you add an ending. The word hop ends in one p after one o, so when you add -ing you write hopping with two p's. Without the second p, hopping would look like hoping, a totally different word. The double letter keeps the short vowel sound where it belongs.

By the end you will spell ten doubled-consonant words and know when the doubling rule fires.

02

Strategy tips

When to double the last letter
  • The base word has one short vowel followed by one consonant. Hop, run, sit, big, hot.
  • You are adding an ending that starts with a vowel. The big three are -ing, -ed, and -est.
  • Both true? Double the last consonant before you add the ending.
  • Hop plus -ing becomes hopping. Sit plus -ing becomes sitting. Big plus -est becomes biggest.
  • Words ending in two consonants do not double. Jump becomes jumping, not jumpping.

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
"All words double their last letter."
No. Only short words with one vowel and one consonant double up. Jump already ends in two consonants, so jumping has one p. Read has two vowels in a row, so reading does not double the d. Check for the short vowel and single consonant before you double.
06

The move

Remember:
  • One short vowel, one consonant, vowel-starting ending. Double the consonant.
  • Without the doubled letter, the vowel sound would change. Hopping turns into hoping.
  • Always check the base word first. Spell it, then add the ending.
