Week 1 Compound Words

A compound word is two real words stuck together to make a new word. Each small word brings its meaning along, so when you read a compound you can often guess what it means by reading each part. Spelling them is just spelling each piece in order, with no letters added and none taken away.

By the end you will see the two parts in ten compound words and spell each one from memory.

What this lesson teachesUse spelling patterns and generalizations to spell compound words.

02

Strategy tips

How to spell a compound word
  • Find the two smaller words. Each one keeps every letter.
  • Say each part as you write it. basket + ball is basketball.
  • Do not add an extra letter at the join. There is no e between butter and fly.
  • Do not drop letters. Snowball has both the w in snow and the b in ball.
  • If a word does not split into two real words, it is probably not a compound.

06

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
"I drop a letter where the words meet."
No. In a true compound, both small words keep every letter. Sun plus shine is sunshine, not sunshine without the n or with two n's. Spell each part fully and you will not lose letters.
07

The move

Remember:
  • A compound word is two real words joined into one.
  • Spell each part fully. Do not add or drop letters at the join.
  • If the parts give a hint about the meaning, use it.
