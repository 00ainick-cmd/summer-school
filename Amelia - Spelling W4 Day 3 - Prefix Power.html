Prefix Power

A prefix is a small piece you stick onto the front of a word. The base word does not change a single letter. You just snap the prefix on. Un-, re-, pre-, and dis- are four of the most useful ones in the third grade. Each one means a different thing, and once you know what each one means, you can guess a whole shelf of new words.

By the end you will spell ten words that use the prefixes un-, re-, pre-, and dis-, and you will know what each prefix does to the base word.

What this lesson teachesUse spelling patterns and generalizations to spell words with prefixes (un-, re-, pre-, dis-).
01

Meet the four prefixes

Each one carries its own meaning. Once you know the meaning, the new word almost reads itself.

un-
means NOT or the opposite
un + safe = unsafe
re-
means AGAIN
re + read = reread
pre-
means BEFORE
pre + heat = preheat
dis-
means NOT or apart
dis + agree = disagree

03

Strategy tips

Spelling moves
  • Spell the prefix first. Two letters for un, re. Three letters for pre, dis.
  • Then spell the base word with no changes. Happy stays happy. Read stays read.
  • Do not double a letter unless the base word already has it. Unsafe is one s. Disappear has two p's because appear starts with a (no change) and dis adds the second.
  • Read it back. Does it mean the prefix meaning? Unsafe = not safe. Reread = read again. Preheat = heat before. Disagree = not agree.

06

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
"I should drop a letter from the base word when I add a prefix."
No. The base word stays the way it is. Unhappy is u, n, h, a, p, p, y. Both p's from happy stay. Reread keeps both r's, both e's, the a, the d. Just spell the prefix, then spell the whole base word.
07

The move

Remember:
  • Un- means NOT. Re- means AGAIN. Pre- means BEFORE. Dis- means NOT or apart.
  • Snap the prefix on the front. The base word does not change.
  • Spell the prefix. Then spell the whole base word. That is the new word.
