Session 06

Integer Exponents

A power is a compact way to write repeated multiplication. Five short rules let you combine, split, and shrink expressions without expanding anything. Today you lock in the five and apply them on sight.

What this session teachesKnow and apply the properties of integer exponents to generate equivalent expressions.
01 / LEARN

The vocabulary, in one pass

In the expression a^n, the value a is the base and n is the exponent. The expression itself is a power. Saying "two to the fifth" means 2^5 = 2 · 2 · 2 · 2 · 2 = 32. The exponent counts copies of the base, not multiplications.

Watch the base. The expression -3^2 means "the negative of three squared," so the value is -9. The expression (-3)^2 applies the exponent to the entire negative, so the value is 9. Parentheses control what gets squared.
02 / LEARN

The three rules that combine and split

When the bases are identical, multiplying powers reduces to adding their exponents. Dividing powers reduces to subtracting them. Raising a power to a power multiplies the exponents. These three rules are the whole reason exponents are useful.

Product: x^3 · x^5 = x^(3+5) = x^8
Quotient: y^9 / y^2 = y^(9-2) = y^7
Power of a power: (z^4)^3 = z^(4·3) = z^12
Same base required. The product and quotient rules only work when the bases match. The expression 2^3 · 5^3 does not equal 10^3 by the product rule; it equals it because of a different rule about products inside a power. Confirm the bases match before adding or subtracting exponents.
03 / LEARN

The zero rule and the negative rule

The rule a^0 = 1 looks strange but follows directly from the quotient rule. Compute a^3 / a^3 two ways. By the quotient rule it equals a^(3-3) = a^0. By plain division any nonzero value over itself equals 1. So a^0 has to be 1 to keep the system consistent.

The rule a^(-n) = 1/a^n follows the same way. Compute a^2 / a^5. The quotient rule gives a^(2-5) = a^(-3). Plain division gives 1/a^3. Negative exponents are a notation for reciprocals.

The base zero exception. The expression 0^0 is undefined, and 0^(-n) is undefined because it would divide by zero. Every rule above assumes the base is not zero.
