Session 01

Real Numbers on the Line

Some numbers can be written as a clean fraction. Some never settle down, no matter how many decimals you write. You will learn to tell them apart, estimate the messy ones, and pin them to a number line.

What this session teachesEstimate the value and compare the size of irrational numbers and approximate their locations on a number line.
01 / LEARN

Two kinds of number

Every number on the line is one of these two. The split comes down to one question: can it be written as a ratio of two integers?

Rational

Can be written as a fraction of integers. Its decimal either stops or repeats forever in a pattern.

3/4 = 0.75 · 1/3 = 0.333... · √16 = 4 · 22/7

Irrational

Cannot be written as a fraction. Its decimal runs forever with no repeating pattern.

√2 = 1.41421... · √5 · pi = 3.14159...

Key move for square roots: if the number under the root is a perfect square (4, 9, 16, 25...), the root is a whole number, so it is rational. If it is not a perfect square, the root is irrational, and it lands between two whole numbers.
