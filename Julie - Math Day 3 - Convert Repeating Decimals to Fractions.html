Session 04

Trap the Repeating Tail

A repeating decimal looks slippery, but one algebra move pins it down as a clean fraction. You will run the move on three examples, then earn the right to do it from memory.

What this session teachesConvert decimals which repeat into fractions and fractions into repeating decimals.
01 / LEARN

The subtraction trick

Let x equal the repeating decimal. Multiply x by a power of 10 chosen so the repeating tails line up. Subtract. The infinite tail cancels, leaving a clean equation you can solve.

Block length 1 digit   multiply by 10
Block length 2 digits   multiply by 100
Block length 3 digits   multiply by 1000
General   n-digit block, multiply by 10^n
Quick consequence. Any 1-digit repeat is some digit over 9. Any 2-digit repeat is some 2-digit number over 99. Any 3-digit repeat is over 999. That is the shortcut, and the algebra below is why.
02 / WALK THROUGH

Three worked examples

Step through each. Watch which factor of 10 you pick, and watch the infinite tail cancel.

03 / DO

Match decimal to fraction

Pick the fraction each repeating decimal collapses to. Reduce when you can.

04 / EXPLAIN

Write the trick in your own words

In three or four sentences, explain why subtracting x from 10x kills the infinite tail. Use the example 0.444... if it helps.

For your thinking, not graded. Read it back. Did you name why the tails cancel?

05 / SAY IT

Out loud, to an adult

Convert 0.818181... to a fraction out loud, step by step. Then state the shortcut: every 2-digit repeat is what over what?

Adult listener. Look for: (1) sets x = 0.818181..., multiplies by 100 to get 100x = 81.818181..., subtracts to get 99x = 81, solves to x = 81/99 = 9/11; (2) names "n-digit repeat over n nines."
