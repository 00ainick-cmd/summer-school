Session 09

Estimating Roots

Most roots are irrational. You will not pull a clean integer from √50, but you can pin it tight between two consecutive whole numbers and then refine the estimate. Today you build that workflow.

What this session teachesInvestigate concepts of square and cube roots.
01 / LEARN

The bracket method

For any square root, find the two consecutive perfect squares that sandwich the radicand. The root then sits between the two square roots of those perfect squares. Here is the workflow.

1

Identify the radicand. Read off the number under the radical symbol.

2

Find the largest perfect square less than or equal to the radicand. Call it L.

3

Find the smallest perfect square greater than or equal to the radicand. Call it H.

4

The root sits between √L and √H, two consecutive integers.

5

Look at where the radicand sits between L and H. Closer to L means closer to the lower integer; closer to H means closer to the upper integer.

02 / LEARN

Three worked examples

Apply the steps. Watch how the bracket appears.

Estimate √50: 49 < 50 < 64, so 7 < √50 < 8. Closer to 49, so √50 is closer to 7. Real value ≈ 7.07.
Estimate √30: 25 < 30 < 36, so 5 < √30 < 6. Closer to 25, so √30 is closer to 5. Real value ≈ 5.48.
Estimate √75: 64 < 75 < 81, so 8 < √75 < 9. Closer to 81, so √75 is closer to 9. Real value ≈ 8.66.
The same idea for cube roots. Use perfect cubes instead of perfect squares. For ∛50, the cubes 27 and 64 bracket 50, so 3 < ∛50 < 4. Real value ≈ 3.68.
03 / LEARN

Comparing irrational values

Estimating lets you order irrational numbers without a calculator. To compare √20 and 4.5, note that 20 is between 16 and 25, so √20 sits between 4 and 5. To get sharper: 20 is closer to 16 than to 25, so √20 is just above 4. That is less than 4.5.

Common slip. Do not assume the root scales linearly with the radicand. Doubling the radicand does not double the root. √4 = 2, but √8 ≈ 2.83, not 4. The squaring relationship is non-linear, so the root grows slower.
