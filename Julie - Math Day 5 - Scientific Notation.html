Session 10

Scientific Notation

A proton weighs about 1.7 × 10^-27 kilograms. The Sun weighs about 2 × 10^30 kilograms. Scientific notation is the tool that fits both into a single sentence and lets you compare them at a glance.

What this session teachesExpress very large and very small quantities in scientific notation and approximate how many times larger one is than the other.
01 / LEARN

The form

Scientific notation writes a number as a coefficient times a power of ten. The form is strict, and the strictness is what makes the notation useful.

a × 10^n
scientific notation
where 1 ≤ a < 10 and n is an integer (positive, negative, or zero).

The coefficient a must have exactly one nonzero digit before the decimal point. The exponent n tells you how many places the decimal would shift to recover the original number. Positive n means a large value; negative n means a small one.

02 / LEARN

Convert in both directions

Standard to scientific

Move the decimal point until exactly one nonzero digit sits to its left. Count the moves. Left moves give a positive exponent; right moves give a negative one.

3,800,000 → 3.8 x 10^60.00042 → 4.2 x 10^-4

Scientific to standard

Shift the decimal point by the exponent. Positive exponent shifts right (and you add zeros if needed); negative exponent shifts left.

6.1 x 10^5 → 610,0009 x 10^-3 → 0.009
Common error. Writing 38 x 10^5 is not scientific notation, because 38 is not between 1 and 10. The correct form is 3.8 x 10^6. Always check the coefficient first.
03 / LEARN

Compare magnitudes

The standard asks you to approximate how many times larger one quantity is than another. For numbers in scientific notation, this reduces to comparing exponents.

A difference of one in the exponent means roughly a factor of ten. A difference of three means about a factor of 1,000. To estimate, subtract the exponents; the result is the order of magnitude difference.

How many times larger is the Sun than a person?
Sun: 2 x 10^30 kg. Person: 7 x 10^1 kg.
Order of magnitude: 30 - 1 = 29. The Sun is about 10^29 times more massive than a person.
When the coefficients differ. A more precise comparison divides the coefficients too. (2 / 7) x 10^29 ≈ 0.29 x 10^29 = 2.9 x 10^28. To the nearest order of magnitude, the Sun is about 10^28 to 10^29 times more massive. For grade-level work, comparing exponents alone is the expected method.
