Session 13

Slope and Y-Intercept

Every non-vertical line in the plane can be written as y = mx + b. The coefficient m is the slope. The constant b is the y-intercept. Two numbers, total control of the line.

What this session teachesApply concepts of slope and y-intercept to graphs, equations and proportional relationships.
01 / LEARN

Slope-intercept form

A linear equation in two variables can be written in slope-intercept form. The form gives you the slope and the y-intercept by direct inspection. No work, no graph drawing. Just read.

y = mx + b
slope-intercept form
where m is the slope and b is the y-coordinate of the point where the line crosses the y-axis.
y = 2x + 3: slope m = 2, y-intercept b = 3. Crosses the y-axis at (0, 3).
y = -x + 7: slope m = -1, y-intercept b = 7. Crosses the y-axis at (0, 7).
02 / LEARN

What the y-intercept means

The y-intercept is the value of y when x = 0. Plug x = 0 into y = mx + b and m times 0 disappears, leaving y = b. So b is always the y-coordinate where the line crosses the y-axis. On a graph, look for the point on the line that sits on the vertical axis; its height is b.

Proportional is the special case. When b = 0 the equation is y = mx, the line passes through the origin, and the relationship is proportional. Proportional relationships are linear relationships with y-intercept zero.
03 / LEARN

What the slope means

The slope m tells you how y changes per unit increase in x. Positive slope means the line climbs left to right; negative slope means it drops. A slope of 0 makes a horizontal line. Larger absolute value means steeper. The sign and size of m completely determine the tilt of the line.

m = 3: climbs 3 units for every 1 unit right.
m = -2: drops 2 units for every 1 unit right.
m = 1/2: climbs 1 unit for every 2 units right.
Read carefully. In y = 5 - 3x the slope is -3, not 5. Rewrite as y = -3x + 5 if the order trips you up.
