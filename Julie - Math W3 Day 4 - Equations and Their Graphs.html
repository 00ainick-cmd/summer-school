Session 14

Equations and Their Graphs

Given an equation, you can predict the shape of its graph before drawing it. Given a graph, you can read off the equation. Slope and y-intercept are the two clues that lock a line to its equation.

What this session teachesApply concepts of slope and y-intercept to graphs, equations and proportional relationships.
01 / LEARN

From equation to graph

Given y = mx + b, you can sketch the line in two steps. First, plot the y-intercept (0, b) on the y-axis. Second, use the slope to step from that point to another. A slope of 3 means go right 1, up 3. A slope of -1/2 means go right 2, down 1. Connect the dots and extend.

y = 2x - 1: plot (0, -1). Step right 1, up 2 to (1, 1). Step right 1, up 2 to (2, 3). Draw the line.
02 / LEARN

From graph to equation

Given a line, you can write its equation in two steps. First, find where the line crosses the y-axis; that y-value is b. Second, pick any other lattice point and use rise over run to compute the slope m. Plug m and b into y = mx + b and you have the equation.

Line through (0, 4) and (2, 0): b = 4. Slope = (0 - 4) / (2 - 0) = -4 / 2 = -2.
Equation: y = -2x + 4.
03 / LEARN

Quick checks that narrow choices

When you have to match an equation to one of several graphs, two fast checks usually decide it. Read each clue off the equation first, then scan the graphs.

Sign of m: positive means the line climbs, negative means it drops. Eliminate any graph that tilts the wrong way.
Value of b: the y-intercept appears as a visible crossing point on the y-axis. Eliminate any graph that crosses at the wrong height.
Two clues, almost always enough. Different lines need different slopes or different intercepts. Pin both and you have the unique match.
