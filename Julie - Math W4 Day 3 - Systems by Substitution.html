Session 18

Systems by Substitution

Substitution turns a two-variable system into a one-variable equation. When one equation is already solved for a variable, you replace that variable in the other equation with the expression it equals. Then you have a single equation in one unknown.

What this session teachesAnalyze and solve systems of linear equations.
01 / LEARN

Why substitution works

If two expressions are equal, you can swap one for the other anywhere they appear. When the first equation says y = x + 1, the symbol y and the expression x + 1 are interchangeable. Replace y in the second equation with x + 1 and the equation now has only x in it.

Pick the easiest setup. Substitution is fastest when one equation is already solved for x or y, or can be solved for one of them with a single move.
02 / LEARN

The four steps

Substitution is the same pattern every time. Solve one equation for a single variable. Substitute that expression into the other equation. Solve the resulting one-variable equation. Substitute the answer back to find the second variable.

System: y = x + 1 and x + y = 5
Substitute: x + (x + 1) = 5
Simplify: 2x + 1 = 5, so 2x = 4, x = 2.
Back-substitute: y = 2 + 1 = 3. Solution: (2, 3).
03 / LEARN

When neither is solved for a variable

If both equations are in standard form, pick the variable with a coefficient of 1 or -1 and isolate it. Avoid coefficients that introduce fractions when you can. Then proceed as before.

System: 2x + y = 10 and 3x - y = 5
Isolate y in the first: y = 10 - 2x
Substitute: 3x - (10 - 2x) = 5 -> 5x - 10 = 5 -> x = 3
Back-substitute: y = 10 - 2(3) = 4. Solution: (3, 4).
Watch the parentheses. When substituting an expression, write parentheses around it. The minus sign in 3x - (10 - 2x) distributes to every term inside, not just the first.
