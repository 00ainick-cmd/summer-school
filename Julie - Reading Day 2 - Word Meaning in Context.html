Session 02

Word Meaning in Context

Skilled readers do not stop reading every time they hit a word they do not know. They read like detectives: lean on the sentence, the affixes, and the figurative move the writer is making. Only after that, if it is still ambiguous, do they reach for a reference.

What this session teachesDetermine the meaning of words and phrases as they are used in the text, including figurative and connotative meanings using context, affixes, or reference materials.
01 / LEARN

Denotation vs connotation

Every word carries two things at once.

Denotation

The literal, dictionary meaning. The thing the word points to.
"Childish" denotes: relating to a child.

Connotation

The emotional or cultural weight the word carries with it.
"Childish" connotes immaturity. "Childlike" connotes wonder. Same root, different feeling.

Writers pick words for both layers. If you only read the denotation, you miss the half of the meaning that is doing the actual work.

02 / LEARN

Figurative language, five common moves

TypeWhat it doesExample
MetaphorCalls one thing another to claim a shared quality."The argument was a wildfire."
SimileCompares using like or as."Her focus was like a laser."
PersonificationGives a nonhuman thing human action or feeling."The wind argued with the door."
IdiomA fixed phrase whose meaning is not literal."Hit the books before finals."
HyperboleDeliberate overstatement for effect."I have asked her a million times."

If the literal reading sounds absurd or impossible, the writer is being figurative. Decode the comparison, then read for the point being made.

03 / LEARN

Four kinds of context clue

DefinitionThe sentence directly defines the word, often after a comma or dash.
ExampleThe sentence lists examples that show the category.
ContrastThe sentence sets up an opposite using but, unlike, however.
InferenceNo direct clue; the meaning has to be inferred from the action or tone.
The reference rule. Context plus affixes (un-, -less, -able, re-, -tion, etc.) handles most words. Reach for a dictionary or reference when the word is still ambiguous after both, or when precision matters: scientific, legal, archaic, or technical vocabulary.
