Session 03

Theme and Its Development

A topic is what a story is about. A theme is what the story is saying about that topic. Skilled readers track theme the way an engineer tracks a load: where it gets introduced, where it gets stressed, where it gets resolved.

What this session teachesUsing appropriate text, determine the theme(s) of a text and analyze its development over the course of a text; provide an objective summary of the text.
01 / LEARN

Topic vs theme vs plot

Three things often get confused. Hold them separate.

Topic

A noun. A subject. "Loyalty." "Power." "Grief." One or two words.

Plot

What happens, in order. The sequence of events.

Theme

A claim about life or human nature, built across the story. A complete sentence.

Most stories carry more than one theme. The strongest theme is the one the writer keeps returning to and pressing against.

02 / LEARN

How theme develops across a text

Theme is rarely stated outright. It develops through four moves.

  • Motifs. A repeated image, object, or phrase. When something keeps appearing, it is doing work.
  • Character arcs. How a character changes (or refuses to change) reveals what the text values.
  • Conflicts. What is at stake, and who or what is pushing back.
  • Resolutions. How the conflict ends carries the writer's claim. A bitter ending and a triumphant ending state opposite themes about the same topic.
03 / LEARN

Objective summary vs personal reaction

Objective
A young runner trains alone for months, places third in the regional final, and decides to keep training for next year.
Not objective
The runner deserved to win, and the ending is sad and a little unfair, but at least she did not quit.

An objective summary reports what happened and what was decided. It leaves out opinion, judgment, and personal feeling. It can name the theme; it should not argue with it.

04 / DO

Track theme across a short excerpt

Read the passage. Then tap the three numbered moments where the theme advances. Each tap explains why it counts.

1For two years, Iniko had run the same three-mile loop alone, before sunrise, before the city was awake to watch her. She was not training for anyone. She was training because the act of returning, day after day, was the only thing that felt entirely hers.

2At the regional invitational, the bleachers were full. Cameras lined the rail. Her coach pressed a note into her palm: Run the way you run when no one is watching. She read it twice and folded it small.

3She placed third. Two runners she had never raced before crossed the line ahead of her, neither of them looking back. A reporter asked if she felt cheated. Iniko thought about it for a long moment. "No," she said. "I came to find out what I could do today. Now I know."

4That night she laid out her shoes for the next morning. The loop was waiting. So was she.

Which three numbered moments advance the theme most clearly?

Pick three. Each correct tap explains its move.
