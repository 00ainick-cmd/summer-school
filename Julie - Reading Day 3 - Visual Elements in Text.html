Session 03

Visual Elements in Text

A chart is not decoration. A photograph is not filler. In informational writing, every visual element advances the argument or supplies evidence the prose cannot deliver as efficiently.

By the end of this session you will read charts, graphs, maps, photographs, and infographics as integrated parts of an expository text, identifying what each visual contributes and how it relates to the written argument.

What this session teaches Interpret visual elements of a text including those from different media and draw conclusions from them (when applicable).
01 / LEARN

Visuals are part of the argument

In sophisticated informational writing, the visuals carry argumentative weight. A bar chart quantifies a trend the prose only describes. A photograph supplies the human evidence a statistic cannot convey. A map locates a phenomenon in space. When a reader skips the visuals, the reader has read only part of the text.

Your job is to identify what the visual contributes and then ask whether it supports, extends, or in rare cases contradicts the surrounding prose.

02 / LEARN

Bar chartCompares quantities across categories.Truncated y-axis can exaggerate differences.
Line graphShows change in a quantity over time.Cherry-picked time window can mislead.
MapLocates a phenomenon geographically; reveals spatial pattern.Color choice can distort severity.
PhotographSupplies human, particular, concrete evidence.Caption framing can change interpretation.
InfographicCombines several data layers into one visual.Density can obscure source and method.
03 / LEARN

Reading a data display: the checklist

Before you draw a conclusion from a chart or graph, run the following four checks. Skipping any one is how readers get misled.

CheckWhat to look for
1. AxesWhat is being measured on each axis? What are the units?
2. ScaleDoes the y-axis start at zero? Is the range honest?
3. SourceWhere do the data come from? When were they collected?
4. WindowWhat time span or sample is shown? What is excluded?

A confident interpretation cites the actual values: "ridership rose from 21,000 in 2021 to 70,000 in 2024," not "ridership rose a lot."
