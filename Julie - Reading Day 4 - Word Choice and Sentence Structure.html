Session 04

Word Choice and Sentence Structure

Two writers can report the exact same fact, and the reader will feel two completely different things. The fact does not change. Diction (word choice) and syntax (sentence structure) do the rest of the work. Hearing that work is what separates a casual reader from a sharp one.

What this session teachesAnalyze how specific word choices and sentence structures contribute to meaning and tone.
01 / LEARN

Diction: the word the writer picked, and the words they did not

Every word the writer chose is a quiet rejection of three or four close synonyms. The choice carries a register (formal or informal), a temperature (warm or cool), and a precision (specific or vague).

Same fact
She walked into the room.
neutral
Swap A
She strode into the room.
confident
Swap B
She crept into the room.
cautious
Swap C
She slunk into the room.
guilty
Swap D
She made her entrance.
theatrical

One verb swap. Five different rooms. That is diction earning its keep.

02 / LEARN

Syntax: how the sentence is built changes how it lands

Short clipped sentences

Speed up the reader. Build urgency, tension, or a hard tone.
She ran. The door slammed. She did not look back.

Long flowing sentences

Slow the reader. Build reflection, mood, or sweep.
She ran through the empty corridor, past the open lockers and the buzzing fluorescent lights, telling herself she would not look back, and then, halfway to the exit, she did.

Parallelism

Repeated grammatical pattern. Builds emphasis and rhythm.
We trained, we lost, we trained harder, we lost again, we kept training.

Fragments

Intentional incomplete sentences. Punch a single idea. Used sparingly.
Quiet at last. For the first time all year.
The test. Read a passage aloud. If your voice naturally speeds up or slows down without you trying, the syntax is doing the work. If it stays flat, the syntax is neutral, and the diction is carrying everything.
