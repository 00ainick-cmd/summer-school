Session 08

Reading Informational with Stamina

A long-form article, a research report, a chapter from a science book. The text is dense, the structure is unfamiliar, and the page count is not small. Stamina here is not about willpower. It is the active-reading protocol that keeps the argument from slipping past you.

By the end of this session you will name the five moves of an active-reading protocol and you will identify the move that protects comprehension in a given moment of dense informational text.

What this session teaches Read and comprehend informational text independently and proficiently.
01 / LEARN

Informational stamina is active, not passive

Dense expository text does not yield to a passive reader. The eye can travel down the column and the mind can register nothing. Stamina in informational reading means deploying a five-step protocol that keeps the reader doing work: predicting, marking, summarizing, questioning, and connecting. Independence and proficiency at this grade level look like a reader who can run this protocol without prompting.

02 / LEARN

The five-step protocol

StepWhat you doWhat it protects
01 PreviewScan title, subheadings, figures, and the first line of each section.Predicts structure; gives your brain a map before reading.
02 AnnotateMark claims, data, and key terms as you read.Forces engagement; creates a trail you can revisit.
03 SummarizeWrite one sentence per section in your own words.Catches comprehension gaps the moment they happen.
04 QuestionNote one thing that does not add up or seems unsupported.Keeps the reader skeptical; prevents passive acceptance.
05 ConnectTie the argument to something you already know.Anchors new information so it sticks.
03 / LEARN

When to deploy which step

The five steps are not a checklist to be raced through. They are tools for distinct moments. Preview is the move you make before reading a single paragraph of the body. Annotate is the move you make while reading. Summarize is the move you make at every section break. Question is the move you make when the prose starts feeling slippery. Connect is the move you make at the end, or whenever you notice the argument touching something you have read before.

MomentStep
Before page one of the bodyPreview
Mid-section, encountering a statisticAnnotate
At a section breakSummarize
Stuck on a confusing claimQuestion
At the close of the articleConnect
