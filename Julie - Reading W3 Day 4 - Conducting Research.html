Session 09

Conducting Research

Research begins with a question worth asking. It moves through credible sources, gathers what each source uniquely offers, synthesizes the findings, and gives credit through standard citation. A well-run research process leaves a trail another reader could follow.

By the end of this session you will name the four stages of a research workflow, identify what makes a source credible, and apply standard citation conventions to integrate evidence honestly.

What this session teaches Conduct research to answer a question (including a self-generated question); gather relevant, credible sources, print and digital; integrate information using a standard citation system.
01 / LEARN

Begin with a question, not a topic

A research project that begins with a topic, such as "climate," tends to wander. A project that begins with a question, such as "how have river floods in the central United States changed since 1990," has direction. A self-generated question pins down what you actually want to know. The question shapes which sources count and which do not.

02 / LEARN

Source types and what they offer

Source typeExamplesBest for
PrimaryInterviews, original documents, datasets.First-hand evidence and direct testimony.
SecondaryPeer-reviewed studies, expert analyses.Reasoned interpretation built on primary evidence.
ReferenceEncyclopedias, government data tables.Established background, vetted figures.

A strong research project draws on more than one type. A single source is a starting point, not a finding.

03 / LEARN

Credibility, on four tests

TestQuestion to ask
AuthorityWho wrote this, and what is their expertise?
AccuracyAre claims cited, verifiable, and free of obvious error?
CurrencyIs the source recent enough for the question being asked?
PurposeIs the source informing or selling? Education or advocacy?

A credible source passes all four. A weak source fails at least one. A blog post by an anonymous author with no citations and a sales pitch fails three at once.

04 / LEARN

Integration through standard citation

Integration means weaving evidence from sources into your own analysis. The standard citation system records who said what, when, and where, so the reader can verify. The two formats most often required in middle school are MLA and APA. The principle is the same in both: every quotation, every paraphrased finding, every datapoint borrowed from a source gets an in-text marker and a full entry in the works-cited list.

05 / DO

Diagnose the source

Each round describes a source a researcher is considering. Select the strongest evaluation of whether the source belongs in the project.
