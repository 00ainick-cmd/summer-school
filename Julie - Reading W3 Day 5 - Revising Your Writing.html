Session 10

Revising Your Writing

A first draft is not the finished thought. Revision is the act of returning to the page with the audience in mind, asking whether the task got done, and rewriting the parts that fell short. The writers who get better are the writers who treat revision as a separate skill.

By the end of this session you will name the three passes of structured revision, distinguish revision from editing, and identify the revision move that fits a given moment in a draft.

What this session teaches Review, revise, and edit writing with consideration for the task, purpose, and audience.
01 / LEARN

Revision is not editing

Revision changes the substance: claims, structure, evidence, voice, paragraph order. Editing changes the surface: spelling, punctuation, agreement, word choice at the sentence level. The two stages are different. Editing a draft that has not been revised is rearranging the chairs on a stage that is still in the wrong room.

02 / LEARN

Three passes, three lenses

PassLensWhat you ask
TaskThe assignment.Does this draft answer the prompt precisely and completely?
PurposeThe argument or aim.Is the central idea clear and supported by real evidence?
AudienceThe reader.Is the tone, register, and level of detail right for the reader?

Each pass produces its own kind of revision mark: cut a paragraph that drifts off task, add a citation that lacks evidence, lower a register that sounds too casual.

03 / LEARN

What revision marks look like

MarkWhat it doesExample
StrikethroughCuts a word, phrase, or sentence."kind of" replaced by "measurably."
InsertionAdds a word, citation, or transition."for instance" inserted to signal an example.
Margin noteFlags a larger change for the next pass."too casual for the assignment" beside a paragraph.
04 / DO

Each round shows a moment in a draft. Select the revision move that most directly fits the task, purpose, or audience problem.
