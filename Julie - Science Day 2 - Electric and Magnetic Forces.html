Session 02

Electric and Magnetic Forces

Two non-contact forces shape almost everything you build: charges that push and pull through empty space, and magnets that do the same with poles. Both follow patterns you can measure. Both fall off fast with distance.

What this session teachesAnalyze diagrams and collect data to determine factors affecting electric and magnetic force strength.
01 / FRAME

Forces without touching

A contact force needs the two objects to touch (your hand pushing a cart). An electric force and a magnetic force act across empty space. You can hold a magnet near a paperclip and lift it without ever touching. The space around a charge or a magnet is called a field. The force on a second object depends on how strong the field is at its location.

Electric

  • Source: electric charge (+ or -)
  • Like charges repel; opposite charges attract
  • Stronger charges, stronger force
  • Force drops fast as distance grows

Magnetic

  • Source: magnet poles (N and S)
  • Like poles repel; opposite poles attract
  • Stronger magnet, stronger force
  • Force drops fast as distance grows
02 / MODEL

The pattern: Coulomb's law

For two point charges, the electric force has a clean formula. Bigger charges scale the force linearly. Distance scales it as an inverse square: double the distance and the force drops to one quarter.

F = k · q1 · q2 / r²

Read it: force is proportional to the product of the two charges, and inversely proportional to the distance squared. Magnets follow a similar shape: stronger poles or closer distance both pump up the force, and the drop-off with distance is steep.

06 / WATCH OUT

Two common mix-ups

Mix-up 1
Doubling the distance halves the force.
Both Coulomb's law and magnetic force fall off as an inverse square, not a straight inverse. Doubling distance cuts the force to a quarter. Tripling cuts it to a ninth.
Mix-up 2
Like things always attract.
For both forces it is the opposite: like repels, opposite attracts. Two north poles push apart. Two positive charges push apart.
