Cycling of matter and energy

In any ecosystem, matter cycles and energy flows. Atoms of carbon, nitrogen, and oxygen are reused indefinitely. Energy, however, enters as sunlight, moves through trophic levels, and is largely lost as heat at each transfer. This session models the difference and walks through producers, consumers, and decomposers.

What this session teachesDevelop a model to describe the cycling of matter and flow of energy among living and nonliving ecosystem parts.
01

Two systems, one ecosystem

An ecosystem runs on two parallel processes. Matter cycles. Energy flows. Confusing these is the most common modeling error.

Matter is conserved. The atoms of carbon, nitrogen, oxygen, phosphorus, and water that build organisms are reused indefinitely. A nitrogen atom in soil today might sit in a plant tomorrow, a rabbit next week, a hawk next month, and back in the soil within a year. Energy, in contrast, enters the system once as sunlight, is converted to chemical bonds in producers, and is dissipated as heat at every transfer. There is no recycling of energy.

Modeling rule. Use solid arrows for energy flow (one direction, ends as heat). Use dashed arrows for matter cycle (closed loop, returns to soil and air).
02

The three trophic roles

Every organism in an ecosystem occupies one of three functional roles based on how it acquires matter and energy.

Producer
Captures solar (or chemical) energy and builds organic matter from inorganic inputs. Plants, algae, and photosynthetic bacteria fix carbon from CO2 and water into glucose. They are the only entry point for energy into the food web.
Consumer
Obtains matter and energy by eating other organisms. Primary consumers (herbivores) eat producers. Secondary and tertiary consumers eat other consumers. Each trophic transfer loses roughly 90 percent of the energy as heat.
Decomposer
Breaks down dead organisms and waste into inorganic compounds. Fungi and bacteria return carbon, nitrogen, and other nutrients to the soil and atmosphere, closing the matter cycle. Without decomposers, matter would accumulate as dead tissue and nutrients would lock up.
03

Matter cycles, energy does not

The distinction between cycling and flow is the heart of this standard. A correct ecosystem model represents them differently.

Matter cycle

  • Atoms are conserved. Same carbon used over and over.
  • Closed loop through soil, atmosphere, producers, consumers, and decomposers.
  • Decomposers are required to close the loop.
  • Examples: carbon cycle, nitrogen cycle, water cycle.

Energy flow

  • One direction: sun, producer, consumer, heat.
  • Open path. Energy enters as sunlight and exits as heat.
  • About 10 percent transferred at each trophic step; about 90 percent lost as heat.
  • No recycling. The sun must keep supplying energy.
Common misconception. "Energy is recycled by decomposers." False. Decomposers cycle matter, not energy. They release low-grade heat as they metabolize, just like every other organism.
04

Walk the model

Trace one carbon atom and one unit of energy through the system shown above.

A carbon atom enters as CO2 from the atmosphere. A plant fixes it during photosynthesis into glucose. A rabbit eats the plant; the carbon now sits in rabbit tissue. A hawk eats the rabbit; the carbon enters hawk tissue. The hawk dies. Fungi and bacteria decompose the body, releasing the carbon as CO2 back to the atmosphere or as organic compounds back to the soil. The atom is available to be fixed again. Loop closed.

A unit of energy enters as sunlight. The plant captures roughly 1 percent of incoming solar energy in chemical bonds. The rabbit obtains roughly 10 percent of what the plant captured; the rest is heat lost in metabolism and movement. The hawk obtains roughly 10 percent of what the rabbit had; again 90 percent is lost as heat. The decomposers metabolize remains and release the last bit as heat. The energy that started as sunlight is now warmth in the air. None of it is reused. The sun must shine again tomorrow.

05

Predict

A scientist removes every decomposer from a sealed terrarium. Sunlight, plants, and a rabbit remain. After several weeks, which prediction best follows from the model?

Decomposers are the matter-cycle closers. Without them, dead tissue accumulates and inorganic nutrients in the soil are not regenerated. Energy still flows from the sun, but the producers eventually cannot acquire the matter they need to grow.
