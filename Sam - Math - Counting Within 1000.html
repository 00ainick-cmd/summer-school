Beat 1 · Hook

The jump decides which digit moves

Two counts start at the same number. One jumps by ten, one by one hundred. Tap each to see which digit moves.

350 → 360
Count on by ten: the tens digit moves.
350 → 450
Count on by one hundred: the hundreds digit moves.
The size of the jump tells you which digit changes. By ones the ones move, by tens the tens move, by hundreds the hundreds move. And at 9, a place rolls over into the next one.
Beat 2 · What you will be able to do

Your goal today

  • 1Count within 1000 by ones, by tens, and by hundreds.
  • 2Start a count at any number, not just a round one.
  • 3Roll over cleanly when a place hits nine, like 199 to 200 or 295 to 305.
Why it matters: counting on and back is how you add and subtract in your head, read a number line, and check money and scores fast.
The objective (verbatim standard)Count within 1000 by 1s, 10s and 100s starting with any number.

Beat 4 · Teach
LEARN 1

The Counting Machine

Press a jump and watch two things: the number on the line, and which digit lights up. Start anywhere. Try +10 a few times, then +1 across a nine, then +100.

Watch for the rollover. When a place is at nine and you add one more, it goes back to zero and the next place up goes up by one. That is how 199 becomes 200.

The jump picks the place

A ten-jump changes the tens digit. 472 becomes 482.
Beat 4 · Teach (start anywhere)
LEARN 3

Begin at any number

Start at 23 and count by tens: 23, 33 ... what comes next?

You can start a skip count anywhere. By tens from 23: 33, then 43.
Beat 4 · Teach (the rollover)
LEARN 4

Crossing into a new hundred

Count on by ten from 295. What comes next?

295 has nine tens. One more ten makes ten tens, which rolls into a new hundred: 305. The tens and the hundreds both move.

Beat 12 · The big takeaway
SYNTHESIS

The one rule to keep

The jump picks the place. At nine, roll over.

By ones move the ones, by tens the tens, by hundreds the hundreds. When a place passes nine, it returns to zero and the next place up goes up by one.
