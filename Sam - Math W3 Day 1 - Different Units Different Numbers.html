Week 3 . Day 1

Different units, different numbers

The same pencil. Two rulers. Two numbers. The pencil did not grow. The unit changed.

By the end, you can tell why one object gives a big number in small units and a small number in big units.

What this lesson teaches Analyze the results of measuring the same object with different units.
01

One pencil. Two answers?

Look up. The pencil is 7 inches. The same pencil is 18 centimeters. Both answers are right.

Inches are bigger steps. Centimeters are smaller steps. When the step is smaller, you need more of them to cover the same length.

The big idea. Small unit gives a big number. Big unit gives a small number.
02

Why the numbers change

A centimeter is small. It takes a lot of centimeters to cover a pencil. So the number is big.

An inch is bigger. It takes fewer inches to cover the same pencil. So the number is small.

The pencil did not get longer or shorter. Only the unit changed.

03

Big unit, small number

Think about your room. You can give the length in feet or in inches.

UnitStep sizeNumber you get
FootBig stepSmall number
InchSmall stepBig number
MeterBig stepSmall number
CentimeterSmall stepBig number
Same room. Different units. 10 feet is the same as 120 inches. The room did not change.
04

Spot the pattern

You measure a desk. You get 3 feet. You measure again in inches. You get 36 inches. Both are the same desk.

Did the desk grow? No. Inches are smaller. So you used more inches than feet.

Rule of thumb: smaller unit, more of them. Bigger unit, fewer of them.

05

Try one

A book is 1 foot long. You measure it in inches. Will the number be bigger or smaller than 1?

An inch is smaller than a foot. So 1 foot is 12 inches. The number gets bigger.
