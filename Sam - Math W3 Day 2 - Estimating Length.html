Week 3 . Day 2

Estimating length

An estimate is a smart guess. You do not need a ruler to guess about how long. You need a unit you trust.

By the end, you can pick a unit and make a smart guess for how long a thing is.

What this lesson teaches Estimate lengths using units of inches, feet, yards, centimeters and meters.
01

What is an estimate

An estimate is a smart guess. It is not the exact answer. It is close to the answer.

A good estimate gets you near the real number. A wild guess is not an estimate.

The big idea. Pick a unit you trust. Compare the object to something you know that size.
02

Pick a body ruler

Your body has built-in rulers. Use them when no ruler is near.

Body partAbout this size
Thumb tip to first knuckle1 inch
Hand from wrist to fingertip6 inches
One foot from heel to toe1 foot (close)
One big step1 yard (close)
Pinky fingernail width1 centimeter

If your hand is 6 inches and a notebook is two hands long, the notebook is about 12 inches.

03

Pick the right unit first

Match the unit to the size of the thing.

ThingGood unit
A bug or a paper clipCentimeters or inches
A pencil, a hand, a phoneInches or centimeters
A door, a kid, a bikeFeet or meters
A driveway, a soccer goalYards or meters
Reminder. Inches and centimeters are for small. Feet, yards, and meters are for big.
04

Smart guess in three steps

Step 1. Pick a unit that fits the size.

Step 2. Compare to a body ruler you know.

Step 3. Give a number that is close, not wild.

A crayon is about as long as a thumb is wide times 4. So a crayon is about 4 inches.

05

Try one

A first grader stands next to a door. Which is a smart guess for how tall the door is?

A door is taller than a kid. About 7 feet is close. 2 feet is too short. 50 feet is way too big.
