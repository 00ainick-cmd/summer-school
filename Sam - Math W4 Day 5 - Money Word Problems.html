Money word problems

A toy costs $1.45. A snack costs $2.10. You buy both. How much do you pay?

By the end, you can read a money word problem, add or subtract, and write the answer with $ and ¢ the right way.

What this lesson teaches Find the value of combinations of dollar bills, quarters, dimes, nickels and pennies, using $ and ¢ appropriately.
01

Read the problem

A money word problem tells a small story. Look for the numbers. Look for the question.

Ask yourself: do I add or take away? "Both" or "in all" usually means add. "How much left" means take away.

Tip. Underline the price words in your head. $1.45. $2.10. Two prices. Time to add.
02

Stack the dollars

When you add money, line up the dot. Dollars over dollars. Cents over cents.

$1.45
+ $2.10
$3.55

5 plus 0 is 5. 4 plus 1 is 5. 1 plus 2 is 3. Total: $3.55.

03

Take away

You have $5. You buy a snack for $2. How much is left?

Take away means subtract. $5 minus $2 is $3. You have $3 left.

Change. The money the cashier gives back is called change. Change equals what you gave minus the price.
04

Worked example

You buy a sticker for 35¢. You hand the cashier 50¢. How much change?

50¢ minus 35¢. Count up from 35: 36, 37, 38, 39, 40, then 50. That is 15¢. Change is 15¢.

05

Try one

A pencil is 25¢. An eraser is 15¢. You buy both. How much in all?

25 plus 15 is 40. Both items together cost 40¢.
