Chapter one

Breaking Big Words Into Bites

A long word looks scary at first, but it is really just little words stuck together. Every word is made of beats called syllables. Find the beats, read each one, and snap them back together. That is the whole trick.

By the end you will clap a word into its beats, split a long word between two consonants, and read each beat using the letter sounds you already know.

What this lesson teachesDecoding multisyllabic words in context by applying common letter-sound correspondences including single letters, consonant blends, consonant and vowel digraphs, and vowel diphthongs.
01

One beat for every vowel sound

A syllable is one beat. Every syllable has one vowel sound. Clap each word and listen.

02

The sounds you already know

You will use these letter-sound friends to read each beat. Tap a card and say the example out loud.

Single letters
one letter, one sound
m says /m/ in map
Blends
two consonants, both heard
bl in blob, st in stop
Digraphs
two letters, one new sound
sh in shop, ch in chip
Vowel teams
two vowels, one team sound
ai in rain, oa in boat
03

Read picnic together

Watch how a long word comes apart and back together.

Look for two consonants in the middle: picnic.
Split between them: pic + nic.
Beat one: p-i-c, pic.
Beat two: n-i-c, nic.
Snap them back: pic-nic, picnic.
04

Split it on the bench

Tap a letter to drop the split line right after it. Try to land between the two middle consonants.

05

Predict the split

Guess the split, then reveal.

06

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
Sound out every single letter, even in a long word.
Letter-by-letter is slow and easy to forget. Find the beats first. Read each beat as a chunk. Then snap them back. Your eyes get to rest between the beats.
07

The move

Remember:
  • One vowel sound = one beat = one syllable.
  • In the middle of a long word, split between two consonants.
  • Use the letter sounds you already know to read each beat.
  • Snap the beats together to say the whole word.
