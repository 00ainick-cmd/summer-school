Chapter one

Find It in the Book

When something in a book is fuzzy, good readers do one thing. They look back. The answer is almost always right there in the text. Point to it. Say it. Now you know.

By the end you will go back to the book to check. You will point to the part that proves your answer. You will use the words from the book.

What this lesson teachesSeeking clarification, and using information/ facts and details about texts and supporting answers with evidence from text.
01

The answer is in the book

Books have all the facts you need. If you forget a fact, go back. The book waits. Open it. Point to the part. Read it again. That is the answer.

Tiny part
Mia has a yellow bike. She rides it to the park every day. Today she wore a blue helmet.

Question: What color is the helmet today?

Look back. The last line says blue. The answer is blue. The proof is right in the book.

02

Three steps for finding evidence

When you are not sure, do these three things.

step 1
Find the question word.
Is it who, what, when, where, why, or how? That tells you what to look for.
step 2
Skim the text.
Run your eyes back over the page. Look for the same key word from the question.
step 3
Point and say it.
Put your finger on the sentence. Say the answer using words from the book.
03

Evidence vs. a guess

An evidence answer comes from the book. You can point to the sentence. A guess comes from your head. You cannot point to it.

Tiny part
Sam packed his red lunch box for school. Inside, he put a ham sandwich and an apple.
Evidence answer
"What did Sam pack?"
A ham sandwich and an apple. The book says so.
Guess
"Sam packed cookies too."
The book never says that. No proof. So it is a guess.
04

Pick the evidence answer

Read each tiny part. Tap the answer that the book proves. Point to the sentence in your head.

05

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
If I think it, it must be the answer.
Not yet. Even if it sounds right, you have to find it. Go back to the book. Point to the part that proves it. No proof means it is a guess. A good reader finds the proof.
06

The move

Remember:
  • When you are not sure, look back at the book.
  • Find the key word from the question.
  • Point to the sentence with the answer.
  • Use the book's words to say the answer.
