Chapter three

Sentences Into Paragraphs

Sentences are like Lego bricks. One brick is not much. Snap a few bricks that fit. You get a paragraph. A paragraph is a group of sentences about one idea. When the idea shifts, start a new paragraph.

By the end you will know what a paragraph is. You will find the topic sentence. You will know when to start a new one.

What this lesson teachesUnderstanding that sentences are organized into paragraphs to convey meaning.
01

What a paragraph is

A paragraph is a group of sentences. They all share one idea. The first sentence is the topic sentence. It tells what the paragraph is about. The rest are detail sentences. They add more about that idea.

Topic sentence: My favorite sport is hockey.

I like to skate fast. I like to think fast and pass to my team. A goal is the best feeling.

Every sentence is about hockey. That makes it one paragraph.

02

New idea, new paragraph

A new idea gets its own paragraph. You can see it on the page. A fresh line. A small indent.

Paragraph 1 · About hockey

My favorite sport is hockey. I like to skate fast. A goal is the best feeling.

Paragraph 2 · About my dog

My dog Lola loves snow. She runs in circles. She bites at the snowflakes. She wants me to throw snowballs.

Two ideas. Two paragraphs. If you mash them, the reader feels lost.

03

Sort six sentences into two paragraphs

Each sentence goes in Paragraph 1 or Paragraph 2. Read it. Tap 1 or 2.

0 of 6 sorted.
04

Find the topic sentence

The topic sentence tells what the whole paragraph is about. Pick it for each one.

05

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
A new paragraph starts every time you press Enter.
No. A new paragraph starts when the idea changes. A paragraph can be long. It can fill a whole page. That is fine if every sentence shares one idea. The real rule is the idea.
06

The move

Remember:
  • A paragraph is sentences about one idea.
  • The topic sentence tells the idea. It is first.
  • New idea = new paragraph.
  • The rest are details about the idea.
