Chapter four

Text Features and Predictions

Before you even read a book, the book is already talking to you. The title, the pictures, the bold words, the headings: those are called text features, and they hand you clues about what is coming. A smart reader uses those clues to predict, then checks against the real words.

By the end you will spot text features, use them to make a prediction, and explain when your prediction was right and when the features tricked you.

What this lesson teachesUsing text features to make and confirm predictions, and explain why a prediction was not confirmed.
01

Text features are the clues on the page

Text features are the parts of a book that are NOT just regular sentences. They are there to help you find your way and guess what is coming.

Title
The big words on the front. Tells you the topic.
Heading
A small title above a section. Tells you what that part is about.
Picture
A drawing or photo. Shows what the text is talking about.
Caption
The little words under a picture. Explains the picture.
Bold word
An important word in dark print. Marks something the writer wants you to notice.
Table of contents
A list of what is in the book and on what page.
02

Predict, then confirm

A prediction is an educated guess. You look at the clues, make your best guess about what the book or page will be about, then start reading. As you read, you confirm: were the clues right?

Sometimes a feature tricks you. Maybe the picture showed a hockey stick, so you guessed the book was about hockey, but really it was about a kid who broke a hockey stick on the first page and the rest is about woodworking. That is fine! You go back, point to the clue, and say "the picture made me think hockey, but the real topic was building things."

03

Read three book covers

Look at the title, heading, picture, and caption on each cover. Make your prediction, then tap "Reveal" to see what the book is really about.

04

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
A prediction is just a wild guess, so any guess is fine.
No. A real prediction uses the clues on the page. "I think this book is about dogs because the title says Lucky the Puppy and the picture shows a dog." If you cannot point to a clue, it is not a prediction; it is just a guess.
05

The move

Remember:
  • Text features (title, heading, picture, caption, bold words) are clues.
  • Predict by using the clues to make an educated guess.
  • Confirm by reading and checking your guess.
  • If you were wrong, point to the feature and say why it pointed you that way.

EXPLAIN

Say it in your own words

Strong work on Form A. Now write one or two sentences: what is a text feature, and how do you use one to make a prediction?

Grown-up note: this part is not graded. A strong answer names at least one feature (title, picture, heading, bold word) and says you use it to guess what the text will be about.
PERFORMANCE

Pick a book and predict out loud

Grab any book you have not read yet. Look at the cover and flip through the first few pages. Then tell an adult: what do you think this book is about, and which features made you think that? Read the first page and tell the adult if you were right.
