Chapter five

Asking Good Questions

Good readers ask questions while they read. Not random questions, the right questions: ones whose answers are in the text or help you understand it. A good question pulls you deeper into the story. A random question floats off into space and leaves the book behind.

By the end you will ask relevant questions while you read, tell the difference between a question that fits the text and one that does not, and find answers right in the words.

What this lesson teachesAsking and responding to relevant questions about a text.
01

Question words

A good question usually starts with one of these. Each one looks for a different kind of answer.

Who
a person or character
What
a thing or event
When
a time
Where
a place
Why
a reason
How
a way it happened
02

Relevant vs. off-topic

A relevant question is one whose answer is in the text or helps you understand it. An off-topic question floats away from the story. Same passage, two very different questions.

Tiny passage
Maya skated onto the ice for her first hockey game. Her hands were shaking, but as soon as the puck dropped she felt brave.
Relevant
"Why were Maya's hands shaking?"
The text tells you: it was her first hockey game.
Off-topic
"What is Maya's favorite color?"
Nothing in the passage tells you, and it does not help you understand the story.
03

Right There vs. Think and Search

Two kinds of good questions, both relevant. Right There questions have the answer in one sentence. Think and Search questions need you to piece the answer together from a few sentences.

Tiny passage
Leo built a snow fort in the backyard. He used a big shovel and packed the snow tight. By the time the sun went down, the fort was taller than Leo.
Right There
"What did Leo use to build the fort?"
One sentence answers it: a big shovel.
Think and Search
"How do we know Leo worked for a long time?"
You piece it together: he worked until sundown, and the fort grew taller than him. That takes time.
04

Pick the most relevant question

Read each short passage. Three questions follow. Tap the one that is most relevant to the passage.

05

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
Any question I think of while reading is a good question.
Not really. If the answer is not in the text and the question does not help you understand the story, it is off-topic. A good question keeps you in the text, not out of it.
06

The move

Remember:
  • Use the question words (who, what, when, where, why, how) to start good questions.
  • A relevant question has its answer in the text or helps you understand it.
  • Right There = answer is in one sentence.
  • Think and Search = answer is pieced together from a few sentences.

Ask three questions out loud

Pick any short passage in a book you have (a page works fine). Read it out loud to an adult. Then ask three relevant questions about it, using three different question words. After each question, point to where the answer is.
