Chapter eight

Helper Words for Verbs

Some sentences use two verbs to tell what is happening. The first one is the helper. The second one is the main verb. I am walking. Am is the helper. Walking is the main one. Today you learn five helpers.

By the end you will know am, is, are, was, and were. You will use them with verbs ending in ing to tell what is going on.

What this lesson teachesUse helping verbs with regular verbs.
01

What is a helper verb?

A helper verb works with the main verb. The helper goes first. The main verb comes after and ends in ing.

see it
Sam is playing. (is is the helper; playing is the main verb.)

The helper tells you who and when. The main verb tells you what.

02

The five helpers

Match the helper to the doer and the time. Three are for right now. Two are for the past.

Doer + time
Helper
I (now)
am
he, she, it (now)

you, we, they (now)
are
I, he, she, it (past)
was
you, we, they (past)
were

Am, is, are = now. Was, were = past.

04

How to pick the helper

Look at the doer first. Then look at the time. Then match.

If the doer is I and time is now, use am.

If the doer is one person or thing (he, she, it) and time is now, use is.

If the doer is more than one or you, and time is now, use are.

For past, the rule is shorter: one person uses was. More than one or you uses were.

05

Pick the right helper

Read each sentence. Tap the helper that fits.

06

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
Saying they is or I are.
More than one needs are. Just I needs am. Match the helper to the doer.
07

The move

Remember:
  • A helper verb plus a main verb in ing tells what is going on.
  • Now: am (for I), is (for he, she, it), are (for you, we, they).
  • Past: was (for I, he, she, it), were (for you, we, they).
  • Match the helper to the doer first.
