Chapter ten

Four Kinds of Sentences

A sentence can tell. It can ask. It can give an order. It can shout big news. Four kinds. Four end marks. The cat is gray. Is that your cat? Pet the cat. I love cats! Same words near the start. Different jobs. Different end marks.

By the end you will know all four kinds. You will pick the right end mark. You will read a sentence and tell which kind it is.

What this lesson teachesProduce simple declarative, imperative, exclamatory, and interrogative sentences.
01

Kind one: a sentence that tells

A telling sentence shares a fact or idea. It ends with a period. The fancy word is declarative.

Declarative
tells you a fact
The sky is blue.
end mark: .

Most sentences you read are this kind.

02

Kind two: a sentence that asks

A question asks for an answer. It ends with a question mark. The fancy word is interrogative.

Interrogative
asks a question
Where is the cat?
end mark: ?

Look for words like who, what, where, when, why, how, is, are. Those start a lot of questions.

03

Kind three: a sentence that gives an order

A command tells you to do something. It usually ends with a period. The fancy word is imperative.

Imperative
gives an order
Close the door.
end mark: .

Notice: the doer is not there. It is you hidden in the sentence. (You) close the door.

04

Kind four: a sentence that shouts

A strong feeling sentence shows a big feeling. It ends with an exclamation mark. The fancy word is exclamatory.

Exclamatory
shows a big feeling
What a great game!
end mark: !

Use this kind for surprise, joy, fear, or shock. Not for every sentence.

05

How to pick the end mark

Read the sentence first. Ask yourself:

the four checks
Does it tell? Put a period.
Does it ask? Put a question mark.
Does it give an order? Put a period.
Does it show a big feeling? Put an exclamation mark.

Two kinds use a period. One uses a question mark. One uses an exclamation mark.

06

Pick the kind

Read each sentence. Tap the kind that fits.

07

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
Using an ! for every sentence.
An exclamation mark is for big feelings. I had pizza for lunch. is a telling sentence, not a shout. Use a period there.
08

The move

Remember:
  • Declarative tells. Ends with a period.
  • Interrogative asks. Ends with a question mark.
  • Imperative gives an order. Ends with a period.
  • Exclamatory shows a big feeling. Ends with an exclamation mark.
