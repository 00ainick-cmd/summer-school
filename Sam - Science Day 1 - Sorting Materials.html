Sorting Materials by What We Observe

Standard 2-PS1-1Plan and conduct an investigation to describe and classify different kinds of materials by their observable properties.
01

Scientists are observers

A scientist looks closely and describes what they notice. The way something looks or feels is called a property. You observe a property with your senses: you look at it with your eyes, and you feel it with your hands. Today you will observe materials, describe them by their properties, and then sort them into groups. That is what real scientists do in a lab. One safety rule: scientists do not taste or smell an unknown material to test it. We observe by looking and by carefully touching.

02

Six properties to know

Each property is a pair of opposites. Keep these in your head.

Hard / Soft
how it feels when you press it
a rock vs a pillow
Smooth / Rough
how it feels when you rub it
glass vs sandpaper
Bendy / Stiff
does it change shape
a rubber band vs a ruler
Shiny / Dull
how light bounces off it
a spoon vs cardboard
Heavy / Light
how much it weighs
a book vs a feather
See-through / Not
can light pass through
a window vs a wall
03

Predict, then check

A scientist guesses first, then observes. Make your prediction, then reveal.

A kitchen sponge. Hard or soft?
Answer: Soft. You can squeeze it, so it is soft.
A glass marble. Smooth or rough?
Answer: Smooth. It slides under your finger with no bumps.
A metal fork. Bendy or stiff?
Answer: Stiff. It keeps its shape and does not bend easily.
A clear water bottle. See-through or not?
Answer: See-through. Light passes through it, so you can see what is inside.
04

Describe one object fully

A scientist picks up a metal spoon. How would they describe it by its properties?
  1. Look at it: it is shiny, and it is not see-through.
  2. Feel it: it is smooth and hard.
  3. Bend it: it is stiff.
  4. Lift it: it is light.
The spoon is shiny, hard, smooth, stiff, and light. One object can have many properties at once.

07

Why scientists sort materials

Sorting is not just a game. Scientists classify materials so they can pick the right one for a job. A window needs to be see-through, so we make it from glass, not wood. A hammer needs to be hard and heavy, so we make it from metal, not foam. When you know a material's properties, you know what it is good for.

08

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
Big things are always heavy.
A big beach ball is light, and a small rock can be heavy. Size and weight are different properties. Observe each one on its own.
09

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
If two things are the same color, they are made of the same material.
Color is a property you can observe, but it does not tell you what a material is made of. A red ball and a red block can be made of very different materials.
10

What you learned

  • A property is something you observe by looking and feeling.
  • Materials have many properties at once.
  • You can sort a group of materials by one property at a time.
  • Scientists classify materials to choose the right one for a job.
