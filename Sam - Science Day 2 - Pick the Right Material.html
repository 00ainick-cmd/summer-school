Pick the Right Material

Standard 2.PS1.A.2Analyze data got from testing different materials to find out which materials have the properties that are best suited for an intended purpose.
01

From sorting to picking

Last time you sorted things by their properties. A property is what you can see. Hard, soft, bendy, stiff, see-through, waterproof, magnetic. Today you do the next step. You pick a thing for a job. You would not make a window from cardboard. You would not make a raincoat from paper. The match between job and stuff is the whole game.

02

03

How engineers choose

An engineer does three things in order. First, say the job. What does this thing have to do? Next, test the stuff. Write down the data. Which ones are bendy? Which ones hold water out? Which ones are strong? Last, make a choice. Pick the one whose properties match the job best.

04

One choice, all the way through

Job: build a spoon for hot soup.
  1. The job needs: stiff to scoop. Strong. Not too soft.
  2. Test: foam is soft. Paper bends in soup. Wood is stiff and strong. Ice melts.
  3. Pick: wood or metal. Both are stiff and strong. Foam and paper fail.
The tags tell you what fits the job. The tags help you pick.

06

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
The strongest stuff is always the best.
A steel window would be strong. But you could not see through it. The best stuff is the one that fits the job, not the strongest of all.
07

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
If it works for one job, it works for every job.
Cardboard is great for a cereal box. It is bad for a raincoat. Each job has its own list of what to look for.
08

What you learned

  • A property is what you can see and test.
  • Engineers say the job. They test the stuff. They pick.
  • The right stuff has properties that fit the job.
  • Strong is not always best. Match beats strong.
