Week 4 . Day 4

Different habitats, different life

A habitat is the home of a plant or an animal. It is the place where it gets what it needs. Each habitat has its own kinds of life. A forest is full of trees and squirrels. A desert is full of cactus and lizards. They are not the same.

By the end you can tell where a plant or animal lives by looking at the habitat.

What this lesson teaches Make observations of plants and animals to compare the diversity of life in different habitats.
01

What is a habitat?

A habitat is the place where a plant or an animal lives. A habitat gives food, water, and shelter. Each habitat is its own kind of place. A forest is wet and shady. A desert is hot and dry. An ocean is salty and deep.

Forest
Many trees, shade, soft soil. Home for deer, squirrels, owls, ferns.
Desert
Hot, dry, sandy. Home for cactus, snakes, lizards, scorpions.
Ocean
Salt water, waves, deep. Home for fish, whales, seaweed, crabs.
Big idea. A habitat shapes what can live there. A cactus would dry out in the ocean. A fish would not last in a desert. Each life form fits its home.

Why this matters. Different habitats have different kinds of life. This is called diversity of life. When you look in a forest you see one set of plants and animals. When you look in a desert you see a whole new set.
03

Why life fits

Each living thing has parts that help it fit its home. A cactus stores water in its stem because rain is rare in the desert. A fish has gills because it needs to breathe under water. A squirrel has sharp claws because it needs to climb trees.

Try this. Watch a backyard for ten minutes. What plants and animals do you see? Write them down. Now imagine that same yard as a desert. How would the list change?
04

Try one

A plant has thick green parts that hold water. It has sharp spines. It can live for weeks with no rain. Which habitat is its home?

A plant that stores water and has spines is built for a desert. Rain is rare there, so the plant holds its own water. Spines keep animals from eating it.
