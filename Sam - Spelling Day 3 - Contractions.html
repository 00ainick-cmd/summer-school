Chapter three

Contractions

A contraction is two words that join into one short word. A little mark called an apostrophe takes the place of the letters that go away. So do not becomes don't. The o in not is gone, and the apostrophe stands in for it.

By the end you will spell ten contractions and know which two words each one came from.

What this lesson teachesuse apostrophes correctly for contractions.
01

This week's words

Each card shows the contraction, the two words it came from, and a sentence using it. The apostrophe always takes the place of a missing letter.

02

Strategy tips

How to spell a contraction
  • Find the two words first. Don't comes from do and not.
  • Drop the letter the apostrophe stands for. In don't, the o in not is gone.
  • Put the apostrophe right where the missing letter used to be.
  • Most contractions end the second word with the apostrophe and one letter, like n't, 'm, 's, or 're.
  • I'm always has a capital I. The other words keep their normal letters.

05

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
"The apostrophe goes between the two words."
No. The apostrophe goes where the letter is missing, not where the two words meet. In can't, the apostrophe is in the spot where the no in cannot was, not between can and not.
06

The move

Remember:
  • A contraction is two words shrunk into one short word.
  • An apostrophe takes the place of the missing letters.
  • Find the two words, drop the missing letter, then put the apostrophe in that spot.
