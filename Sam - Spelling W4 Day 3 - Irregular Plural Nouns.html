Irregular plural nouns

Most plurals are easy. One cat, two cats. One dog, two dogs. You just add an s. But a few words break the rule. One foot. Two ... feet. One mouse. Two ... mice. These tricky plurals are the ones to learn by heart.

By the end you can spell the irregular plural for ten common words.

What this lesson teaches Form and use frequently occurring irregular plural nouns (e.g., feet, children, teeth, mice, fish).
01

The plural rule

Most words add s to mean more than one. Cat becomes cats. Book becomes books. But some words do not follow that rule. The word itself changes. These are the ten you should know by heart.

Notice the patterns
  • The "oo" in foot and tooth turns into "ee" for feet and teeth.
  • The "a" in man and woman turns into "e" for men and women.
  • Mouse changes a lot. It becomes mice.
  • Child grows a whole new ending. It becomes children.
  • A few words like fish and deer stay the same in the plural.

03

Pick the right plural

Read the sentence. Pick the plural that fits. The right answer is not always the s ending.

04

Watch out for this

Common mix-up
"Just add s to every word."
No. The s rule works for most words. But foot, tooth, man, woman, mouse, child, person, and goose all break it. Saying "foots" or "childs" is wrong. The right plurals are feet, teeth, men, women, mice, children, people, and geese. Some words like fish and deer do not change at all.
05

The move

Remember:
  • Most plurals add s. But some words change in a special way.
  • foot -> feet, tooth -> teeth, man -> men, woman -> women.
  • mouse -> mice, child -> children, person -> people, goose -> geese.
  • fish stays fish. deer stays deer. sheep stays sheep.
